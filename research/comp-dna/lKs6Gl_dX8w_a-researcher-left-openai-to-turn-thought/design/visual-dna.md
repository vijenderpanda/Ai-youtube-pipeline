# Visual DNA — lKs6Gl_dX8w "A researcher left OpenAI to turn thoughts into text"

## Archetype
"Doc-essay hook + kinetic-text explainer" — talking-head host intercut with dense caption cards, research-paper screenshots, and moody sci-fi b-roll (not a face-cam vlog, not a UI-screen-record tutorial).

## Hook mechanic (first 3s)
Bold claim card ("OpenAI Researcher Just Quit") over host's face, paired with a red Akatsuki-cloud-style channel bug bottom-left for instant brand recognition. Immediately cuts to a mystery visual (person in odd headset) before any explanation — curiosity gap, not proof-first.

## Caption grammar
Two registers used deliberately:
1. Kinetic short-phrase captions (2-4 words, large, center or lower-third) during hook/reveal beats — drip-feeds a sentence across cuts.
2. Dense paragraph captions (small, upper or lower block, near-verbatim narration burned in) during context/proof beats — reads like a Notion/article page, sustains information-heavy stretches.
No animated word-by-word karaoke; captions are static per shot, changing on cut.

## Information density
High — narration is essayistic (full sentences, citations, named researchers/papers), captions often reproduce entire paragraphs on screen. Relies on viewer pausing/rereading or trusting VO; not optimized for silent scroll like caption-only shorts.

## Reusable asset components
- **Wordmark reveal card**: centered logo/name on flat bg, minimal, used to "name" the product/company.
- **Kinetic label chip**: single word/short phrase centered on flat cream bg, cuts rapidly.
- **Pull-quote card**: large quote text on flat bg, attributed headline style ("Why I'm leaving X to build Y").
- **Citation/paper screenshot card**: mock research-paper or press-article layout with dense body text + headline, establishes credibility.
- **Brand comparison chip row**: small product/brand cards (logo + label) laid in a grid to imply competitive landscape.
- **Abstract data-viz insert**: wireframe body scan, brain MRI/CT, node-graph diagrams — used as metaphor cutaways between talking segments.
- **Persistent channel bug**: small red circular logo, bottom-left, present across many but not all frames — brand continuity marker.
- **Dark tension corridor shot**: desaturated teal/black hallway or empty-desk composition with big all-caps kinetic word — used for stakes-raising mid-video beat.

## Colors → content mapping notes
- Flat cream/off-white (#F2F1EC-ish) bg = information/text cards (calm, editorial, "reading" mode).
- Near-black/desaturated teal = tension, stakes, mystery b-roll (dark hallway, empty desk, "SEARCHING" card).
- Cyan/electric blue = clinical/scientific visualization (CT scans, brain graphics) — signals "hard science."
- Red (accent) = channel brand bug + occasional dramatic close-up (blood/surgical) — used sparingly, high-impact only.
- Host wardrobe is neutral (white/cream hoodie, black top) so it never competes with card colors.

## Weaknesses / what NOT to copy
- Caption paragraphs are too dense/small to read at Shorts speed on first watch — relies on rewatch or audio; risky for silent-scroll audiences.
- Very long for a Short (111s) with a slow multi-stage build — high probability of drop-off before payoff on cold traffic.
- Ending is ambiguous/unresolved rather than a clear CTA or clean payoff line — good for curiosity, bad for a clear answerable-in-2s promise.
- Heavy reliance on stock-feeling b-roll (empty offices, generic MRI graphics) that isn't uniquely produced — replicable but not distinctive per se.
- Inconsistent channel-bug placement (present early/late, absent mid-video) — weakens brand recall if copied loosely.
