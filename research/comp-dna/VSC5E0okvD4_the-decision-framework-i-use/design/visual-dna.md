# Visual DNA — VSC5E0okvD4 "The Decision Framework I Use" (Greg Isenberg)

## Archetype
Kinetic-icon explainer — no host, no b-roll, no screen-recording. Entirely vector-icon + typographic motion graphics (silhouette figures, geometric shapes, simple UI mockups) over a neutral vignette. Closest to "animated whiteboard concept video."

## Hook mechanic (first 3s)
Zero text. A single stick-figure vector icon mid-action (throwing/catching) with a fast morph into scattering tile fragments. Pure motion + shape curiosity — forces the eye to track movement before any claim is read. No spoken-word captions burned in until ~t=14s.

## Caption grammar
- One serif italic word/phrase per beat, centered, appearing/scaling in sync with narration.
- Emphasis words (the "punch" word of the sentence, e.g. "busy", "momentum", "Hell yeah") set in bold italic and often a different color weight than the rest of the line.
- Lines build progressively: partial line shown, then completed ("that's when" → "that's when you realize").
- No caption background bar, no stroke/outline — relies on high value-contrast against the flat cream bg, not on a caption plate.
- Very sparse — many beats (0-9s, 45-49s) run with NO text at all, letting the icon carry the beat alone.

## Information density
Low text density, high concept density. Roughly 1 short phrase per 3-5s beat; never more than 6-8 words on screen at once. Each abstract claim is paired with one clean icon (funnel, checklist, node diagram) rather than paragraphs — density comes from rapid beat-to-beat cutting (~1 new visual/sec), not from word count.

## Reusable asset components
- **Silhouette actor icon** — flat teal human silhouette used for metaphor scenes (throwing, shouting, buried, glowing) instead of a real host.
- **Citation card** — circular headshot photo + name + title, used once to borrow authority (portable: swap photo/name per guest/quote).
- **App/phone mockup frame** — rounded phone outline with an animating checklist inside; makes advice feel like a tool.
- **Checklist/checkbox row** — labeled rows (Time/Focus/Energy) that check then strike through; reusable "decision test" component.
- **Funnel/inverted-triangle filter icon** — visualizes narrowing/filtering concepts.
- **Node/hub-spoke diagram** — center circle + 2 labeled spokes, used to reveal a "framework" (Trust/Distribution around Decision making).
- **Momentum cradle (Newton's cradle) icon** — visualizes reset/momentum concepts.
- **Coin/cash-stack icon family** — for money/value beats, includes ripple pulse variant.
- **Scrabble-tile word build** — individual letter tiles that assemble a punch word ("HELL YEAH") for a payoff beat.

## Colors → content mapping notes
- Base is a warm off-white/cream radial vignette (#EDE9E1-ish), constant across the whole video — never goes dark, never goes full color photo.
- Single accent teal/sea-green (#3E8C74 mid, #1F5E4C deep) carries every icon, all captions in default state, and the citation card ring. No secondary accent color is used — red appears exactly once, only for the "no" strike-through slash (t=16s), reserved purely as a rejection/negation marker.
- Monochrome-plus-one-accent palette keeps it feeling premium/editorial rather than colorful/kids-y, and makes every asset trivially re-themeable by swapping one hue.

## Weaknesses / what NOT to copy
- Very low replay/re-watch value for anyone who already knows "hell yeah or no" — first 15s is a well-known meme-adjacent quote, may cause knowledgeable viewers to bounce.
- Several beats (icon-only, no caption) rely entirely on narration audio to land the point — with sound off, meaning is not decodable (bad for silent-autoplay feeds).
- Text/word choice is quite abstract ("Bandwidth ≠ Time") — high risk of losing viewers who need concrete numbers/examples rather than metaphor.
- No overt CTA/subscribe beat and no channel branding visible anywhere in the sampled frames — relies entirely on platform/algorithm for follow-through, nothing to copy for our own CTA needs.
- Pacing is narration-locked (near-1-cut-per-second) — hard to reuse the exact rhythm without matching VO cadence precisely; copying the cut rate without matching VO will feel frantic or empty.
