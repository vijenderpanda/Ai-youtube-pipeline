## Archetype
"talking-head tech-explainer + product demo B-roll collage" — host narrates in short fragments over cutting between his own face, macro product shots, screen-recording proof, manufacturing footage, and diagram inserts. Not a screen-record walkthrough; the host stays the spine, everything else is proof cutaway.

## Hook mechanic (first 3s)
Mid-sentence open ("You can now...using...") with zero logo/intro card — deliberately withholds the subject. Cuts immediately to an ambiguous mouth close-up before revealing the product, creating a 4-second curiosity gap before the payoff ("THE [product]"). No number/superlative in the hook — pure mystery-object curiosity gap.

## Caption grammar
2-4 word fragments per cut, one clause split across 3-6 consecutive captions (reads like the sentence typed out one phrase per beat). Captions are terse, lower-third, plain sans-serif, no heavy styling/emoji/color-coding — text is a transcript aid, not a graphic element. Quote marks used once for a rhetorical aside ("weird," the best...).

## Information density
High — a new visual asset or claim roughly every 1s (9-10 cuts/10s). Claims stack additively (capability → analogy → compatibility list → manufacturing → science → disclaimer → twist → bonus) rather than repeating one point. Compatibility/logo-card beats (Windows/macOS/Linux/Android/iOS) compress 5 claims into 2 seconds via plain white text cards — cheap, scalable info-density technique.

## Reusable asset components
- **White wordmark/logo interstitial card** — plain background + centered serif word or brand mark, used as a section-break beat (repeatable for any product reveal or compatibility list).
- **Multi-app icon grid overlay** — grid of app icons over host b-roll to claim "works across everything" without separate demo footage of each app.
- **Screen-recording proof-of-use inlay** — small window/PIP of an actual desktop/app being controlled, embedded over a person-using-product wide shot.
- **Manufacturing/process macro sequence** — scan → mold → print shots in quick succession = "custom made, real R&D" trust block.
- **Anatomical/technical diagram card** — labeled illustration (palate/sensor/tongue) as an authority beat; swappable for any "how it works inside the body/device" explanation.
- **Split-frame dual-speaker interview** — two co-founder headshots stacked for a quick corroboration/quote beat.
- **Compatibility logo list card** — plain text/logo, one platform per card, rapid-fire.
- **Disclaimer/proof-grid card** — small-multiple thumbnail grid + fine-print honesty text near the end.

## Colors → content mapping notes
Base palette is near-monochrome (near-black footage, white interstitial cards) — color is used sparingly and functionally: warm skin tones dominate live footage; brand red (hoodie graphic, likely brand accent) recurs on host's shirt across the video as an implicit brand-color anchor; one teal-tinted screen-recording insert (~t=22-24s) breaks the palette, likely just source footage color, not intentional branding. Diagrams use a soft coral/peach for anatomy = safe, non-clinical medical color choice.

## Weaknesses / what NOT to copy
- Cut rate (near 1 cut/sec) is very fast; may feel chaotic to less-produced channels without matching footage volume — don't force this pace without enough B-roll assets.
- Several beats depend on genuinely unique real footage (medical scan imagery, manufacturing floor, second speaker) that can't be faked cheaply — don't attempt this archetype without real proof assets or it reads as stock-footage padding.
- Caption styling is plain/unbranded — no strong signature look to copy directly; the DNA here is structural (pacing, claim order) not typographic.
- CTA is soft/absent (ends on a joke, no explicit follow/comment ask) — fine for a brand awareness video, weak model if the goal is subscriber conversion.
- Teal color-graded insert clip is inconsistent with the rest of the near-monochrome grade — looks like an unintentional continuity slip, not a technique to replicate.
