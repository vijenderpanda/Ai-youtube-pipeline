## Archetype
"Annotated static infographic + stylus-pointer walkthrough" (a single printed/designed poster image that a hand with a stylus circles/underlines/taps over ~70s, VO-narrated, then hard-cuts to a dark countdown-timer sales card). No host, no b-roll, no screen recording — it's essentially a zoomed, hand-annotated slide.

## Hook mechanic (first 3s)
Opens directly on the full payoff claim already rendered in the poster ("Mastering Claude in 2026 = worth 1 Crore+ and even more!") with a hand entering frame mid-underline. No spoken cold-open beat, no face — the poster itself IS the hook, doing double duty as thumbnail-like static image + information density. Big red currency number + orange highlight card up top is the scroll-stopper.

## Caption grammar
Short 2-4 word burned-in caption chips (white bold text on dark semi-transparent rounded rect), one phrase at a time, roughly matching each spoken clause ("MASTERING CLAUDE", "IN 2026 IS", "WORTH 1 CRORE+", "WHO ACTUALLY KNOW", "AND BY KNOWING", "I DON'T MEAN"...). Captions track VO almost word-for-word (near-verbatim, not summarized), placed low-center over the poster, distinct from the poster's own printed text above it.

## Information density
Very high — the underlying poster is a dense one-page diagram (5 columns x multiple rows of icons/labels/checkmarks) normally meant for a static feed post, repurposed as 87s of video by having a hand slowly reveal/annotate sections. Density is front-loaded visually (everything is on screen at t=0) but paced out verbally/by annotation order — classic "reveal-what's-already-there" trick to stretch a poster into a full short.

## Reusable asset components
- "5-column ladder diagram" — numbered level/step columns with icon + short label + checklist, top-anchored headers (LEVEL 1..5)
- "Stat/claim header card" — bordered box, giant colored number + supporting label (e.g. "1 CRORE+")
- "Icon + label chip" — small square icon (line-art) with caption beneath (Gmail/Drive/Excel style tool icons)
- "Crossed-out price ladder" — descending prices (2000→500→100) each struck through, ending in a red "FREE" stamp/circle
- "2-option comparison card" — split left/right panel with icon-led checklist rows, "OR" divider circle
- "Persistent CTA banner" — fixed top strip with icon + "REGISTER NOW" that stays pinned once introduced
- "Dark glass countdown card" — glassmorphic rounded card, neon accent headline, large mm:ss/s:ss timer, CTA button — reusable as a standalone end-card component independent of the main poster
- "Hand/stylus annotation layer" — simulated live-marking (circle, underline, arrow, tap) driving pacing without any real motion graphics

## Colors → content mapping notes
- White bg + near-black text = neutral "professional infographic" base, keeps it feeling like a legit course document, not an ad
- Red/burnt-orange (#C0392B / #E8912A) reserved for: money/value claims, urgency circles, crossed-out prices, "FREE" stamp — i.e. color = "pay attention, this affects your wallet"
- Countdown card flips to a totally different dark-green/neon palette (#0D1A0D bg, #C7F92E accent) — deliberate palette break signals "this part is different: act now," separating educational content from the raw sales close

## Weaknesses / what NOT to copy
- Zero real motion/camera — pure static image + fake stylus annotation for 70s is monotonous audio-only-carries-it; risky without a genuinely strong, tightly-scripted VO
- No host/face at all — no parasocial trust-building, purely info+sales; works only because content (career/salary claim) is inherently high-stakes/attention-grabbing
- Bait-and-switch structure: frames itself as free educational content but ~40% of runtime (49s-87s) is a hard workshop sales pitch — retention will fall off a cliff for viewers who wanted the tutorial, not the upsell; don't copy the length/ratio of the sales tail
- Price-anchoring theatrics (2000→500→100→FREE) reads as generic "webinar funnel" tactics — feels dated/spammy versus native YouTube content; use sparingly if credibility matters more than raw conversion
- Persistent REGISTER NOW banner from 70s onward permanently occupies screen real estate over the content — clutters the frame for the remaining annotation beats
