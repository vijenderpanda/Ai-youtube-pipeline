# Visual DNA — 7A4s1UtOYv0 "Stop paying for AI, NVIDIA made every top AI FREE"

## Archetype
Talking-head explainer + screenshot/UI proof-cuts ("news anchor + evidence reel"). Static seated host as narrative spine, cut every ~1-2s to real product screenshots, logo stings, and cinematic b-roll that illustrate each claim as it's spoken.

## Hook mechanic (first 3s)
Cold open on a specific, painful price ("Stop paying $20") — no channel intro, no logo bumper. Immediately cuts to real app UI as visual proof within 2-3s. The hook is a cost/pain claim, not a question or curiosity gap; it works because $20 is a number the AI-tool audience already recognizes (subscription fatigue).

## Caption grammar
- One short phrase per cut, 2-6 words, revealed in sync with speech (not full sentences at once).
- White bold sans, black stroke/shadow for contrast against any background.
- Centered horizontally, sits in the lower-middle third (chest-height, below chin).
- Selective word-level recoloring for the single most important word/phrase per beat (lime-yellow or lime-green) — e.g. "Max", "unfair advantage", "real products". Recolor = emphasis cue, used ~1x per 8-10s, not every line.
- Text on cream cards uses plain black monospace/serif-ish, no recolor — those cards ARE the visual, text is secondary.

## Information density
High. New visual asset roughly every 1-2s (screenshot, logo, b-roll, or card) even though host audio is continuous. Host talking-head is the "filler"/connective tissue; screenshots/cards are where the actual claims get proven. No single shot holds longer than ~4-5s except the host default shot. Text-on-screen is sparse (short phrases) even though visual cutting is dense — density comes from B-roll variety, not from wall-of-text.

## Reusable asset components
1. **Host full-frame talking-head** — static seated chest-up shot, warm blurred bookshelf bg; the "anchor" shot returned to between every proof beat.
2. **App/UI screenshot insert** — real product screenshot or screen-recording, full-bleed vertical crop, used as literal evidence for a claim.
3. **Logo sting card** — single brand/model logo centered on solid dark bg (Anthropic wordmark, GLM, whale/DeepSeek), used for rapid-fire "these are the options" sequences.
4. **Concept card (cream/khaki slab)** — flat light-colored card with one short black-text sentence, used to distill an abstract idea (business-model split) into a simple visual beat. Distinct color from the rest of the video = signals "pay attention, key idea."
5. **Danger/tension color-flash** — brief red-tinted overlay on a shot, used once to mark an emotional escalation beat ("developers panicked").
6. **Authority photo insert** — press/keynote photo of a named exec (Jensen Huang) used as credibility proof for a claim about the company.
7. **Cinematic b-roll breather** — abstract nature/forest or generic coder b-roll dropped in between dense technical steps as a pacing breath.
8. **Off-platform CTA screenshot** — phone mockup of the destination (WhatsApp community) shown literally, not just described, right before the ask.

## Colors → content mapping notes
- Near-black warm background = default/neutral (host shot, most of runtime).
- Lime-yellow/lime-green = "pay attention, this is the payoff word" — reserved, not overused.
- Cream/khaki card = "conceptual explainer" mode, signals a shift from narrative to diagram-like thinking.
- Red flash = negative/tension emotion (frustration, panic), used exactly once — a scarce resource so it hits harder.
- Native app/brand colors preserved in all screenshots — never re-skinned, which keeps the proof feeling authentic/unstaged.

## Weaknesses / what NOT to copy
- Screenshot inserts are sometimes shown too briefly/small to actually read (viewer must trust host's paraphrase, not verify) — a cloned version should hold proof shots long enough to be legible or crop into the specific relevant region.
- Some mid-video screenshots (model catalog list at 36-42s) repeat near-identical UI for multiple beats — slight monotony/could be tightened.
- The urgency/scarcity CTA ("NVIDIA will tighten this soon") is a somewhat generic scare tactic not tied to a real deadline — reuse the CTA screenshot/off-platform funnel mechanic, but ground urgency claims in something verifiable if cloning for a compliance-sensitive channel.
- Host framing never varies (same seat, same crop, same lighting) for 77s — fine for a fast-cut format but would feel static in a slower-paced video.
