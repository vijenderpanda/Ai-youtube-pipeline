## Archetype
Face-cam monologue, zero cuts, zero b-roll — "talking-head aphorism" format. One static lockoff shot for the full 27s, no edits at all beyond caption overlays.

## Hook mechanic (first 3s)
No visual hook — the hook is entirely verbal/textual: an unfinished conditional sentence ("The next time you open a document and you think AI could help,") that forces the viewer to keep watching to resolve the clause. No pattern-interrupt cut, no on-screen text shock, no zoom. Extremely low-production-value hook that relies purely on premise/curiosity gap and the host's direct-to-camera delivery energy.

## Caption grammar
- 2-line max caption blocks, bottom ~20% of frame, tight word wrap.
- All-caps, heavy/black weight sans, white fill + black stroke/shadow for legibility over any background.
- Yellow (#e8e13a-ish) word-level highlight on 1-2 emphasis words per line (usually the semantically loaded noun/verb: HELP, UPLOAD, WITH, PRIVACY, INTELLIGENCE, FRICTIONLESS, NEEDS, THE, CONVENIENCE).
- Captions update roughly every 1s (word-by-word or short-phrase burn-in), tightly synced to speech — classic auto-caption-tool cadence (Opus/CapCut style), not hand-designed.
- No caption background plate/box — text sits directly on video with stroke only.

## Information density
Very low visual density, moderate verbal density. One idea per ~3-4s beat, delivered as a single continuous spoken argument (no lists, no on-screen diagrams, no UI proof). All teaching is done through spoken word + emphasis captions; the viewer never sees an actual demonstration of the technique (e.g., no screenshot of "smaller copy" or a redaction tool). Density comes from precise, aphoristic sentence construction, not from screen content.

## Reusable asset components
- **Caption emphasis burn-in**: word/phrase captions with single-color keyword highlight — themeable, easy to port (font, 2 colors, position).
- **Static host-lockoff frame**: chest-up host, laptop-in-shot desk setup, neutral bookshelf backdrop — a "credibility set" component, not really a designed UI asset.
- No numbered-step cards, no app-window frames, no quote slabs, no before/after splits, no progress pills — this video has ZERO of the usual cookbook UI components. It is caption-only.

## Colors → content mapping notes
- Background is native environment (warm wood bookshelf, blue polo) — not a designed brand palette, just "creator's real office."
- Yellow highlight = the only intentional brand-color choice, used purely for caption emphasis, not tied to content type.
- No accent2/secondary color use visible anywhere in-frame.

## Weaknesses / what NOT to copy
- Zero visual proof of the technique being taught — pure talking-head with no demo, screenshot, or diagram. Weak for tutorial/how-to retention since there's nothing to "watch happen."
- No explicit on-screen CTA or channel branding visible in the sampled frames — relies entirely on platform UI (follow button) for conversion.
- Single static shot for 27s risks visual monotony; only survives because of caption pacing and host's gesture energy — a lower-energy host would sink this format.
- Hook has no visual component at all — purely a bet on the opening sentence; risky for cold-audience scroll-stop compared to formats with a visual pattern-interrupt in frame 1.
- No lower-third identity/name tag — unclear who's talking without prior channel familiarity (hurts new-viewer trust/authority signal).
