# Visual DNA — VE2Uxb9Fz7I "Claude Cowork Explained"

## Archetype
Kinetic-text explainer with illustrated desktop/app metaphors + real mock-UI screenshot proof beats. No host, no face-cam (except two brief real reaction-face inserts near the twist). Motion-graphics driven, not talking-head driven.

## Hook mechanic (first 3s)
Brand mascot icon on a radiating dark-green starburst background, morphing into the product's orange lightning-bolt icon within 2 cuts. No spoken words needed on screen — pure logo-motion + hard color cut into a terminal screen at t=3s signals "this is technical, watch." Fast (1s) cut cadence from frame 0 establishes pace immediately.

## Caption grammar
Not karaoke-word captions. Design-copy headlines: bold, all-caps for emphasis words only, 2-5 words max per screen, centered or left third, on flat cream or dark-green backgrounds. Proof beats (chat/UI screenshots) carry small dense body text rendered as realistic app content, not captions. Idiom-as-image gag ("elephant in the room" → literal wooden elephant) substitutes for a caption line entirely.

## Information density
Very high cut rate (~1 new visual per second) but each individual screen is low-text/low-density — the density comes from rapid beat-switching, not from any single crowded frame. UI-mock screens (13-19s, 23-28s) are the exception: those carry paragraph-length "proof" text meant to be paused/read, functioning as credibility inserts rather than fast beats.

## Reusable asset components
- "App icon on radiating starburst" bumper (intro/brand reveal)
- "Desktop + monitor + permission-dialog" illustration (app requesting access)
- "Prompt input bar with placeholder + submit button" (literal ask-it-to-do-things UI)
- "Chat reasoning + rendered data table" proof card (AI showing its work with structured output)
- "File result card" (icon + filename label, e.g. spreadsheet card)
- "Red glowing node network" diagram (app-to-app reach/capability map)
- "Mini app-window mockup" (email compose, Slack search — generic recognizable chrome)
- "Word-built headline" (2-4 words land one at a time, bold serif/sans mix)
- "Idiom-object sight gag" (abstract phrase rendered as a literal 3D object)
- "Branching decision path" (icon splits into two colored lines to two outcomes)
- "Vertical safety slider/toggle" (orange-to-green gradient bar with a draggable marker)
- "Approve/deny choice card" (question + two options each with x/check icon)
- "Icon-to-icon connector loop" (two app logos linked by an infinity/dashed line — integration payoff)

## Colors → content mapping notes
- Dark green (#1F3A34-ish) = brand/intro/tension/danger-adjacent beats (starburst intro, node network, twist transition)
- Cream/off-white (#EAE6DD-ish) = calm/explainer/proof beats (headlines, UI mockups, desktop illustrations)
- Orange (#E8623D-ish) = the product accent — used for the lightning-bolt icon, CTA buttons, permission-alert block, danger end of the safety slider, and emphasis words in headlines
- Muted green/teal (#5B9279-ish) = secondary accent on desks/monitors and safe end of the safety slider
- Color pairing (cream + dark-green split screen) is used specifically at reframe/twist moments (t=4s, t=43s)

## Weaknesses / what NOT to copy
- No spoken-word burned captions at all — relies entirely on viewer reading fast-changing on-screen text; risky for sound-off/distracted scroll unless VO is very tightly synced to the beat cuts (per our own retention law, this format needs a strong 5-7s hold hook since there's no karaoke caption to re-anchor attention)
- Two real-photo inserts (t=35-36s) break the otherwise 100%-illustrated system jarringly — looks like stock reaction images, lowest-craft moment in the video
- Some proof-text screens (13-18s) are too dense to read at 1 frame/sec pace on a first watch — only works if paired with narration reading it aloud simultaneously
- "BUT NOT ALL THAT'S" (t=42s) is a truncated/garbled transition line, ambiguous phrasing — copy quality dips right before the CTA beat
- Ending is soft/aspirational ("possibilities are fascinating") with no explicit CTA, subscribe prompt, or next-step — matches this channel's brand voice but would underperform for our answerable-in-2s CTA law
