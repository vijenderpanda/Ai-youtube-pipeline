## Archetype
Talking-head explainer with heavy mixed-media cutaways (archival photos, illustrated comic panels, chalk-diagram inserts, product photography, abstract data-viz). Base shot is a locked, static camera on one host — everything else is inserted B-roll doing the persuasive work.

## Hook mechanic (first 3s)
Silent-beat pose (host with hands clasped, contemplative/praying gesture) held under a stacked quote-card text overlay, before the spoken hook line lands as burned-in caption ("AI could have done what Einstein did?"). The pose creates a stillness/sincerity cue that contrasts with the high cut-rate that follows — sets up "serious question" framing before the fast-cut proof barrage starts.

## Caption grammar
- Every spoken beat gets a 1-2 line burned-in caption, white fill + heavy black stroke/shadow, bold condensed sans, sentence case, no terminal punctuation.
- Captions sit lower-third/lower-center, never overlapping the host's face or hand gestures.
- Captions update roughly every 1-2s in sync with speech — near word-level pacing during talking-head segments; captions disappear entirely during illustration/photo cutaways (image + spoken VO carries the beat instead).

## Information density
High. A claim is almost never left on the talking-head shot alone — nearly every factual assertion (protein folding, math proof, Einstein's marriage/context, a named drug, immune-system mechanism) gets its own dedicated visual within 1-3s of being spoken. Average shot length on cutaways is short (1-2s); talking-head holds run slightly longer (2-4s) between insert bursts.

## Reusable asset components
1. **Quote/question card** — stacked semi-transparent text boxes over a blurred/static bg, used for the cold-open hook.
2. **Black-bg statement card** — full black background, centered white title text (e.g. "Help Science"), used as a chapter-break/gravity beat.
3. **News-chyron card** — boxed source-logo + headline text mimicking a tech-news screenshot, used to inject third-party credibility.
4. **Archival photo insert** — B&W/sepia toned historical photo, sometimes duotone-processed to color, used for authority-figure callbacks.
5. **Chalk/hand-diagram insert** — live handwritten equation or line-diagram on a dark chalkboard-style card, used for mechanism explanation.
6. **Illustrated comic panel** — flat-color (blue-toned) editorial illustration style for case-study narration, run as a short sequence (3-5 panels) rather than a single still.
7. **Product photography insert** — real packaging/bottle photo shot on a plain background, used to make an abstract claim concrete.
8. **Macro/close-up photo insert** — close detail photo (e.g. skin reaction) used as a visceral punctuation beat.
9. **Abstract data-viz card** — wireframe/plot-style graphic paired with a technical claim (protein folding, math proof) to visually "prove" a numeric/technical statement without real data being shown.
10. **Wordmark/logo sting card** — glitch-styled topic wordmark ("Science") used as a mid/late callback beat.

## Colors → content mapping notes
- Sage-green + warm wood talking-head set = the constant "home base" the video always returns to.
- Red accent object on the shelf is the only saturated color in the base shot — functions as a passive brand-color anchor without being a graphic overlay.
- Cutaways each carry their own palette (archival = B&W/sepia, illustration = flat blue, product photo = neutral studio, data-viz = white/blue line-art, black cards = pure black/white) — color is used to signal "you have left the host's world" for each insert type, not kept consistent across cutaways.

## Weaknesses / what NOT to copy
- No CTA card or explicit end-screen — the video just stops on a talking-head line; relies entirely on spoken tease, which is a weaker/harder-to-verify pattern for our template than a graphic CTA.
- Very cutaway-heavy pacing requires a large asset library per episode (10+ distinct B-roll pieces) — expensive to replicate exactly; not viable if we only have host + captions + 1-2 cookbook components per beat.
- Several cutaways (photo of Einstein's wife, product bottle photography) depend on stock/archival footage licensing that we cannot safely reproduce — treat as "inspiration for a themeable component category" (e.g. our own AmbroXol-style photo → generic "product proof card"), never copy the actual asset.
- Caption typography looks like a stock CapCut/Premiere bold-white-outline preset — recognizable but generic; don't treat it as a differentiated style worth copying exactly, just note the grammar (short lines, synced, lower-third).
