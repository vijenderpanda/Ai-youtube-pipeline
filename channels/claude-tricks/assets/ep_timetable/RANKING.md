## Why it beats the runners-up

**It is the only idea where the scarcity theme and the artifact theme are the same shot.** VJ asked for scarcity made visible *or* a tool the viewer walks away holding. Forty grey tiles becoming eleven bright ones is scarcity as pure geometry, and the thing left on screen is a timetable you screenshot. Nothing else on the board does both in one mechanism.

**Muted legibility is the highest score I gave anything (10).** Many → few → objects that can't land → objects made to fit. No caption is load-bearing at any point. At 2x with the sound off in a bright room it still reads. Compare Scam Checker, whose entire argument is a colour interpolation, and Gmail, whose argument is two slabs bouncing apart — both need the VO to convert motion into meaning.

**4–8s is genuinely earned, not scheduled.** A 1.2s domino ripple with countable clicks starting at 4.0, a hard cut and digit change at exactly 6.00, then chips seating from 6.3 — every one of those five seconds has a distinct mechanical event, and the 6.00 beat also *reverses the eye's travel direction*. That is the strongest defence of the worst measured second in the set, and unlike Chat Summary or Free Limit, the repair does not weaken it — the Sat/Sun swap *increases* the kill count from 29 to 29 real days and makes the ripple longer.

**Its repair makes the video more true and more dramatic at the same time.** Every other FIXABLE in the top five pays for honesty with a weaker frame: Chat Summary loses its reversal, Free Limit loses its lockout, Packing List loses its scrollbar, Used Phone loses its off-frame band whips, Resume Cleaner has to reassign its hero surfaces. Here the fix (swap "no study during school hours" for "I can only study Sat and Sun") converts an invented ratio into arithmetic the viewer can redo on their own phone calendar — and the honest version deletes *more* tiles.

**Zero fragile truth.** No plan-availability that can change overnight (Gmail), no model-behaviour that may not reproduce (Scam Checker's beat), no tax adjacency (Rent Receipt), no trademark surface (Used Phone), no five-year privacy claim needing exact policy wording (Resume Cleaner). The only fact in it is a calendar and a prompt, and both are checkable in ten minutes on the render morning. On a 13-hour clock with a 00:30 IST publish, that matters more than any half-point on the scorecard.

**The overflow beat is the channel's differentiator rendered as motion.** Fourteen chapters that do not fit into eleven days, hovering amber with nowhere to land, then three days cracking into halves — a real limitation shown as a feature, with conserved arithmetic (11 days, 3 splits, 14 slots, 14 chips). No competitor short does that; every other one-line-app video hides the constraint.

**Against the two closest runners-up specifically.** Punched Card (84) is the more inventive component and I would build it next, but "Study Plan" is the weaker searched noun of the same pair, its first four seconds needed rebuilding, and shipping both study ideas within a week cannibalises the noun. Used Phone Checklist (84) has an equally good sting and the best single transform on the board (unspool-to-list), but its repair demands the bands be re-originated from a document panel, it needs three new components instead of two, and it carries a live trademark constraint on a rendered handset — the wrong bet against a 13-hour clock.

**Cannibalisation call:** shelve the EchoArc Study Timetable (80) permanently as a separate episode and fold its revision-arc payoff into a later re-cut; ship Punched Card Study Plan no sooner than 7 days out, retitled off "Study".
