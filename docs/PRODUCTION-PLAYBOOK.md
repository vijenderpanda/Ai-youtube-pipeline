## 0. Two production pipelines (pick per channel)
- **Pipeline A — Kids music video** (Made-for-Kids, RPM ~$0.30–1): song → Suno Pro → Leonardo stills (fixed-seed consistency) → FFmpeg motion → word overlays → upload → multi-language dubs. Powers Pip/Lulla, Poly, Rumble.
- **Pipeline B — Avatar explainer** (NOT-MFK, RPM ~$8–25 + affiliate): script → ElevenLabs voice → real host (HeyGen) + REAL screen footage → Remotion motion design → ducked master → upload. Powers AI Unpacked. **This is the "v2 premium" pipeline documented below.**

---

## 1. Core doctrine (all channels)
1. **Premium = process, not tool output.** A signature host/character + real proof + a locked visual system. Raw generator output always reads as slop.
2. **Human-in-the-loop** — never full autopilot (YouTube July-2025 inauthentic-content rule). Automate render/metadata/upload; a human owns the character, the script, and a 60-sec QC gate.
3. **Quality > cadence until the format proves.** Ship 3–4 agency-grade/week over 7 mediocre; accelerate to daily only once it lands. (Creators earn daily *after* the machine matures.)
4. **Clarity beats spectacle** (esp. education). Don't chase a rival's hype energy you can't match — win on "I finally understood it." Narrate-what-you-see: say the on-screen action *at the moment it happens*.
5. **Verify reality at production time.** Fetch feeds/WebSearch before scripting anything time-sensitive. If a feature is only announced-not-live, say so and make a "this is coming" teaser — never state stale facts as current.
6. **Match a reference creator's STRUCTURE + energy, never their face/identity.** ("Vaibhav-style" = desk framing, command hooks, fast delivery, punch-ins — the *format*, not the person.)

## 3. Audio mastering formula (the recipe + the bugs that cost us)
The exact chain that turns quiet TTS + a music bed into a broadcast-loud, voice-forward mix:
```
[music] volume=0.35, afade=t=in:st=0:d=1.5           # bed fades up from silence
[vo]    volume=11dB, asplit=2 [v1][v2]               # TTS is ~12dB quiet; boost it
[music][v2] sidechaincompress=threshold=0.05:ratio=8:attack=40:release=600  # music DUCKS under voice
[v1][ducked] amix=inputs=2:duration=first:normalize=0                       # normalize=0 or amix HALVES both
                , alimiter=limit=0.89                # tame peaks to ~broadcast
```
**Bugs that burned real time (don't repeat):**
- **`volume=0.35` is a RATIO, not a level — level-match any substituted bed first (AI Unpacked Ep24, 2026-08-05).** The 0.35 was calibrated against one specific bed (`bed_active`, −8.3 LUFS programme level). Swap in a different track and the whole master silently moves: Ep24's approved bed was `bed_3` at −13.7 LUFS and the master landed **−21.1 LUFS**, 2.2 LU under the channel's shipped −18.8/−18.9 spine — inaudible as a bug, obvious as "why is this one quiet". Fix is a pre-gain equal to the programme-level delta (`volume=<Δ>dB` ahead of the locked `volume=0.35`), never an edit to the chain topology. **Make `ffmpeg -i out.mp4 -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -` against the *last shipped master* a mandatory gate** — compare integrated LUFS, and also a VO-present window (it should match to ~0.1 LU; if the integrated matches but the VO window doesn't, the duck is wrong, not the bed).
- **A bed's programme level is not its programme level over the 30s you actually use (AI Unpacked Ep26, 2026-08-05).** The channel bed `bed_active` measures **−8.3 LUFS whole-file** — the number the locked `volume=0.35` was calibrated on — but **−10.8 LUFS over its first 30 seconds**, and a Short never hears anything else. Ep26 used the standing bed, changed nothing, and still landed **−19.5 LUFS**, 1.4 LU under the spine. The first diagnosis (a 0.8 LU quieter VO render) was **wrong** and its fix moved the master 0.1 LU — the giveaway was that the raw VO's *hook* was actually 0.5 LU **louder** than the reference episode's while the mastered hook was 2.5 LU quieter, i.e. the missing energy was the bed, not the voice. **Measure both beds over `-t <episode duration>`, not whole-file, before computing any `music_gain_db`** (+1.9 dB here → −18.2 LUFS, dead centre of the −18.1/−18.8/−18.9 spine). Corollary: when a level fix barely moves the meter, the diagnosis is wrong — do not stack a second gain on top of it, revert and re-measure.
- 🔴 **The loudness reference is the LAST SHIPPED master, measured — not this doc's prose (AI Unpacked Ep25 v4, 2026-08-05).** The "−18.8/−18.9 spine" above is real but **stale**: it predates ep24 (−18.1) and ep26 (−18.4, the last actually-uploaded master, `youtu.be/BlQ-gDzw1aE`), so an episode "corrected" toward −18.8 would land 0.4 LU *under* the file it is supposed to match. Before setting `music_gain_db`, `ffmpeg -af ebur128` the newest master that is genuinely **published** (check `CONTENT-CALENDAR.csv` for `SHIPPED`, not the newest mtime in `renders/`) and target that ±0.3 LU. Ep25 v4 landed **−18.6** at `music_gain_db 3.0`, deliberately: it is within 0.3 LU of the measured reference *and* of the older spine, so it satisfies both readings. Sensitivity on this chain is ~**0.4 LU per 1 dB** of bed pre-gain — but **that figure is bed- and duration-specific, not a constant, so treat a correction as a bisect step, not a solve** (AI Unpacked #27, 2026-08-06: a +1.7 dB pre-gain moved a 39s master **1.1 LU**, −19.1 → −18.0, i.e. ~0.65 LU/dB; backing off by the measured overshoot/0.65 to +1.1 dB landed −18.4, an exact match to the shipped reference). Re-measure after every change and let the meter, not the predicted slope, decide when you are done. Also **re-measure after any tail change**: trimming quiet frames (a shortened sting) moves the integrated on its own.
- **The bed-pre-gain sensitivity is NOT a constant — measure it per episode, and treat any published figure as a starting guess only.** Three measurements now exist on the same locked chain: ~0.4 LU/dB (playbook original), ~0.65 LU/dB (Ep26→27, 39s episode), ~0.56 LU/dB (Ep28, 30s episode: −19.0 → −18.5 for +0.9 dB). It moves with episode duration and bed slice, so a one-shot correction computed off someone else's number will land close but not on. Compute the first correction from the newest figure, then **re-measure the delivered file and stop when it is within 0.3 LU of the reference** rather than iterating toward an exact match.
- **Compare final-to-final, and don't over-read a cross-episode window.** The VO-present-window check (~0.1 LU) is for the *same* mix topology; across episodes it is not apples-to-apple — Ep25 v4's 10–18s window reads 0.8 LU under ep26's because ep26's LRA is 5.1 LU against v4's 1.7 (different beat structure, not a broken duck). Integrated on the delivered `*_outro`/`*_final` file is the gate; the window is a diagnostic.
- `amix` **halves** its inputs by default → always `normalize=0` (music was inaudible for a whole review cycle).
- You **can't reuse a filter label** in one graph → `asplit` the VO before feeding two branches.
- A `str.replace()` that **silently no-ops** if the pattern doesn't match, paired with an **unconditional "success" print**, shipped a "musicless" cut to review. **Always `assert pattern in s` before replacing; never print success without verifying.**
- Homebrew's `ffmpeg` here was built **without libass** (no `subtitles` filter) → render captions as PNG overlays (Pillow) or in Remotion, not ASS burn.

## 4. Captions & motion design (premium sizing)
- **Small, ~78px, bottom-third, 1–3 words at a time**, heavy black outline, spring-pop in. **NOT** giant mid-frame pills — those read as amateur/thumbnail blocks (direct user feedback).
- **Anton** (condensed display) is the shorts-standard face — bundle it and load via `@font-face` + `staticFile`.
- Hot/key words alternate **magenta ↔ yellow**, slight ±3° tilt for punch.
- **Step chips** ("STEP 1/3 → 3/3") slide in during demo beats = the comprehension scaffold that makes a how-to followable.
  - **Placement rule (user feedback, Ep11 2026-08-04): the chip must NEVER cover the content the beat is teaching.** 1920-native terminal recordings start their text at the very top-left — exactly where the default chip sits (it covered the command being typed). `steps` tuples now take an optional 4th field `pos` (`tl`/`tr`/`bl`/`br`, Short.tsx) — per beat, put the chip in the corner with dead space, or leave headroom in the tape. QC every demo beat for chip-over-content before shipping.
  - **A corner chosen from a PROXY clip is not a decision — re-probe it against the shipped clip before render (AI Unpacked Ep24, 2026-08-05).** In a staged/asset-first pipeline the overlay-placement asset often lands *before* the footage it describes, so it picks a corner from a stand-in ("same site, same recorder, same camera arc"). Ep24's chip 3 was probed on a proxy and scored `tr` best; on the real clip `tr` was the **worst** corner — the re-ask prompt bubble sits top-right and *is* the "same question" proof the beat teaches. Layout intuition transfers between clips; **the specific pixels do not.** Also re-derive the chip's *time window* from the measured VO + sidecars, not from a predicted beat map — a superseded recording moved Ep24's `seed_typing_start` by 1.9s, which alone invalidates every probed frame.
  - **Stop hand-probing corners — `scripts/probe_frames.py corner CLIP --window T0 T1 --label "…"` scores all four off the real frames** (built Ep26, 2026-08-05). It mirrors `StepChip`'s geometry from `Short.tsx` (40px inset, `top:170` / `bottom:560`, 76px tall, width from the label) and returns the ink fraction inside each corner rect across the window; 0.00 is the only score that fully satisfies the rule. Sibling mode `probe_frames.py ink CLIP --window T0 T1 [--region …]` returns the widest safe punch zoom + center for §10c.
  - **The score ranks corners; it does not decide. Read WHAT the ink is (Ep25 v4, 2026-08-05).** On a dashboard beat no corner scores 0.00, and the winner by score can be the wrong corner: chip 3 measured `tl` 0.0749 vs `tr` 0.0814 — a 0.7pp difference — but `tl`'s ink is the asset title + green APPROVED badge, i.e. *the review surface the beat is teaching*, while `tr`'s ink is a truncated metadata tag row. Ship `tr`. When every corner is dirty, rank by score, then pick the one whose ink is **chrome** (header tails, metadata, watermark band) over the one whose ink is **content**, and record both numbers so the judgement is auditable.
  - 🔴 **Probe the window you will CUT, not a padded one (Ep26).** The same chip probed over 42.5–46.5s scored `tl` **0.13** and named `br` the winner; over the 40.0–43.7s the episode actually cuts, `tl` scored **0.00**. The extra 2.8s ran past the prompt submit, where the page scrolls and completely different pixels sit in the corner. Derive the probe window from the built spec's segment (`build_ep_v2.py --dry` first), never from a guess around the beat.
  - **The rule generalizes past step chips (found on AI Unpacked Ep23, 2026-08-05):** any overlay — `emphasis` text, captions, a chip — can cover whatever's on screen underneath it, not just a demo recording. An in-house data-mock (e.g. a price-bars graphic) has its own labels that an `emphasis` line can blot out just as easily as a chip blots out a terminal command. QC every overlay against whatever beat is live under it, every time, not only step chips against recordings.
- **ALL in-episode stat/price/comparison charts use the locked `StatBars` Remotion component (`remotion-studio/src/components/StatBars.tsx`) — NEVER improvise chart layout (owner QC, AI Unpacked Ep23 2026-08-05).** Ep23 pre-baked a 1080×1080 PIL bar card and played it through the newsSplit pane, where `objectFit: cover` cropped ~420px off EACH side: labels amputated left/right, fat bars. The spec is locked inside the component, not in per-episode code: ≥64px 9:16-safe side margins (`SAFE_X=72`), max 6 bars, bar width ≤42% of slot pitch (abs cap 116px — thin, rounded caps), staggered spring grow-in, value labels with above/inside-top collision handling so they can never clip, channel-accent gradient fill, optional ±delta pills. Feed data via props and render it INSIDE the comp at native 1080×1920 (`StatBarsDemo` in `Root.tsx` = reference usage + QC harness). Pre-baking square/odd-aspect chart images into a cover-fit pane is banned. **The v2 episode DSL declares chart beats natively:** `stat:<name>` in `beats` (optional `|mode` suffix) + props under `cfg["stats"][<name>]` in `build_ep_v2.py` → emits a `{"kind": "statBars", "stat": {...}}` segment; `build_ep_v2.py --ep NN --dry` regenerates the spec JSON without rendering for QC diffs (Ep23 migrated off its PIL card this way, 2026-08-05).
- 🔴 **`StatBars` drew its own axis labels INSIDE the caption band — a locked component can still be wrong, and the fix belongs in the component (AI Unpacked #27, 2026-08-06).** The baseline was `height*0.76` (y1459 at 1920) and the bar labels sat at `baseline + 26` = **y1485**, i.e. inside the y1440–1517 caption band *by construction*, on every chart this component would ever draw — #27 caught the caption "TOTALS" sitting on the Q2/Q3 axis labels and the feet of both bars. Baseline is now **0.70** (labels y1370–1406, ~34px of clearance) at a cost of ~11% plot height. Two rules generalize: (1) the §4 escape hatch "fix the camera, not the caption" has **no camera** on a beat drawn in-comp — the equivalent lever is the component's own vertical rhythm; (2) "locked" means *locked against per-episode improvisation*, not against correction — a layout bug gets fixed once at the system level so every future beat inherits it, exactly like `Cover`'s auto-split. Re-QC any older episode with a `stat:` beat against this.
- **A 0.00 corner score is "no ink in the rect", not "reads clean" (AI Unpacked #27).** `probe_frames.py corner` scored `bl` at 0.00 for #27's chips 2–3, and the rendered frame showed the chip landing ~25px under the ILLUSTRATIVE disclosure — legal by the metric, crowded to the eye, on the one element whose entire purpose is that it can never be covered or ambiguous. Ep25 v4's rule ("the score ranks corners; it does not decide") extends past *content vs chrome* to **proximity**: after ranking, look at what the chip is now sitting next to.
- 🔴 **The caption band itself can cover the proof — and the fix is the camera, not the caption (AI Unpacked Ep26, 2026-08-05).** Captions sit at `bottom: 21%` (≈y 1440–1517). On a text-dense demo that is not empty space: Ep26's payoff beat had the human rewrite's last line under the word "NOW", i.e. the caption covering the exact sentence the beat exists to prove. Never move the locked caption position to fix this — **raise the punch camera's center so the content lifts out of the band** (0.438 → 0.524 at zoom 1.05 lifted it ~90px; only the card's empty bottom padding still meets the band). Note the ceiling: `style_punch` clamps the crop into frame, so the highest usable center is `1 − 1/(2·zoom)` — if that is not enough lift, raise the zoom (staying under the measured horizontal ink cap), don't fight the clamp. Prose the beat is *illustrating* rather than teaching (a wall of formal email) may sit under a caption; the sentence being proved may not.
- Punch-in zooms on hard cuts (open ~1.08× → settle) for energy.
- **Karaoke lyric sync (music videos, learned on Aashiqana 2026-08-03).** User rejected estimated line timings ("captions go fast, audio behind") and wanted per-word coloring. The reliable pipeline:
  1. **Isolate the vocal** — `demucs -d cpu --two-stems=vocals song.mp3` (MPS errors → force `-d cpu`; ~9min for 3.5min track). Whisper on the mixed song mis-times or drops lines; on the **clean vocal stem** it nails onsets. A cheaper first pass is a vocal-forward EQ (`pan mono, highpass 180, lowpass 6000, acompressor, +6dB`) but demucs is far better.
  2. **Word timestamps** — `faster-whisper small, word_timestamps=True, vad_filter, condition_on_previous_text=False` on the stem → group words into line windows `[s,e]`.
  3. **Karaoke render** (`scripts/lyric_karaoke.py`): per line render TWO identical PNGs (white + gold, same scrim/geometry), make a clip via `xfade=transition=wiperight:duration=D` (white→gold = left-to-right fill; **wiperight not wipeleft** — wipeleft fills from the right), `setpts=PTS+{s}/TB`, overlay with `enable='between(...)'`. Keep the lyric pass SEPARATE from the montage so re-syncing never re-renders the base.
  4. **Gaps stay clean** — only emit lines within sung windows; instrumental breaks show nothing.
  - Gotcha: an ffmpeg encode that dies leaves a **0-byte/moov-less mp4**; a chained `-c copy` slice of it fails with "moov atom not found" — check the producer's exit before consuming its output.
  - **Hand-estimated line windows DRIFT** (user: "captions chase the audio, starts smooth then goes off"). Whisper *word onsets* fix line STARTS but the real fix is **forced alignment**: `stable-ts` (`load_faster_whisper('small').align(vocal_stem, DEVANAGARI_lyrics, language='hi')`) → per-word times for the exact lyric, in order. Since gold sweep is per-line, chunk aligned words back into lines by word-count → line `[s,e]`. Align **Devanagari** (matches the sung language) even when displaying **romanized** — same word order, apply times to the roman line. Then **(a) onset-snap** each start to the nearest VAD vocal-onset within ~2.5s (guarded so it never crosses the previous line) to fix the ~2s error right after instrumental gaps where the aligner interpolates, and **(b) sweep-cap** each line's end to `min(aligned_e, next_s-0.2, s+6.5)` so lines the aligner stretched across a break don't crawl and gaps stay clean. Install note: `stable-ts` needs `torchaudio` matching `torch` (ABI `_aoti_torch_abi_version` error → `pip install torchaudio==<torch ver>`); `demucs` MPS-errors → force `-d cpu`. `medium` align model times out >10min on CPU for 3.4min → use `small`, run backgrounded.
  - 🔴 **Silero VAD at threshold 0.5 misses sung attacks buried in reverb — take the UNION over thresholds (0.5, 0.35), `speech_pad_ms=0`, `min_silence_duration_ms=120` (Aashiqana hook cut, 2026-08-05).** Sung vocals with a wet tail sit below speech confidence, so 0.5 alone silently dropped two real onsets (8.26 and 18.85 on the 29.6s hook) and the aligner's early starts survived unsnapped. `speech_pad_ms` defaults to **400** — leave it and every onset arrives 0.4s early; `min_silence` defaults to **2000ms** and merges whole verses into one region, destroying the per-phrase onsets you are snapping to. **Always verify each snap against the stem's own 20ms RMS envelope** — a correct snap sits on a visible step (−29 dB → −19 dB in one or two frames); if there is no step, the snap is fiction.
  - 🔴 **Where VAD finds no onset at all, onset-snap CANNOT fix the aligner — re-align that line in isolation.** A line inside a continuous sung wash (held vowel + reverb never crossing the speech floor) has no attack to snap to, which is exactly where the whole-cut aligner interpolates worst: on the Aashiqana hook, F0 sat locked at ~262 Hz from 24.6→27.65s and `meri wafaa` was placed **0.48s late**. Re-aligning that one line alone over the remaining audio (`realign_isolated()` in `scripts/align_karaoke.py`) removes the pull of the neighbouring segments and recovered the true 27.06. Corroborate before trusting it: a **free, no-lyric-prior** whisper transcription of the tail should start its first word at the same time, and phonetics should agree (a note change at the next syllable; a voiceless fricative shows as the low band collapsing ~13 dB while 2–6 kHz holds).
  - 🔴 **An inherited line window is a hypothesis about a NEW bed — re-verify its onset against that bed's own 20ms RMS envelope (Aashiqana lyric duel, 2026-08-06).** The duel's audio is the same 155.28s origin as the Aug-5 hook cut (confirmed by envelope cross-correlation: 0.991 / 1.000), so `timing_hookcut.json`'s forced-aligned windows transfer sample-for-sample — and that is exactly the argument that stops you checking. Line 2's inherited start was **8.00**; the bed's envelope steps **−11.3 → −7.3 dB in one 20ms frame at 7.92**, so the aligner was 80ms late and a picture cut placed on it would land *after* the attack the viewer hears. Cheap to check, invisible if you don't: a hard cut 80ms behind the downbeat reads as a slightly loose edit and nobody can say why. Cut the picture on the measured attack (7.90), not on the inherited number.
  - **A caption whose vocal begins under a title card must appear PART-FILLED — never re-anchor the sweep to the reveal (`vis_s` in `lyric_karaoke.py`, 2026-08-06).** When a frame-zero card owns the first ~2s and the line has been singing under it, there are two wrong fixes: start the sweep at the reveal (the gold then lags every word by the length of the card) or shrink the card to a 1.2s flash (the §13 "gone by ~1s" failure). `vis_s` is a third field that gates only the overlay's `enable` window while the xfade clip stays PTS-anchored to the true line start, so the line arrives with its gold already ~30% across — which is the truth: the gold is a record of what has already been sung, not an animation that has to start when you look.
  - **Give the aligner a sink and expect its last segment to fail.** A `(parenthesised)` align-only line that runs past the cut edge will itself fail to align (stable-ts warns `N/M segments failed to align` and collapses it to the clip end) — that is fine and is the point: it absorbs the sustained tail so the last *displayed* line is not stretched to the end of the cut. Check the warning names only the sink.

## 9. Publishing infra & hygiene
- `scripts/yt_upload.py` — **one gcloud/GCP project + one OAuth client, one token per channel** (`secrets/token_<key>.json`). Flags: `--synthetic`, `--publish-at <RFC3339 UTC>`, `--audience`, `--category`, `--thumbnail`, `--playlist`.
- 🔴 **NEVER let a tool persist a scope the user hasn't consented to — it bricks the whole network** (measured 6 Aug 2026). `Credentials.from_authorized_user_file(tp, SCOPES)` stamps the *aspirational* `SCOPES` list onto the object, and `get_creds()` then wrote it back with an unconditional `to_json()`. So the first run of any tool after `force-ssl` joined `SCOPES` rewrote three token files claiming a scope that was never granted; the next refresh asked Google for it against a grant that lacked it and died **`invalid_scope: Bad Request`** — taking down uploads, `network_stats.py`, `verify_uploads.py` and `daily_check.py` at once, silently, whenever each token happened to expire. The two rules: **load with the token's own scopes** (`from_authorized_user_file(tp)`, no scope list) and **only write the token file when the credentials actually changed** (fresh consent or a real refresh). Adding a scope to `SCOPES` is a *request made at consent time*, never a fact about an existing token. Repairing a poisoned token needs no re-auth — drop the ungranted scope from the file's `scopes` list and the existing refresh token works again.
- **A channel key is not always its token filename.** Poly is keyed `language-abc` in `UPLOAD_DEFAULTS` / `channels/` but was authorized as `poly` → `secrets/token_poly.json`. `yt_upload.TOKEN_ALIASES` holds the exceptions (`lulla`/`pip` → `token.json`, `language-abc` → `token_poly.json`); without it every network-wide tool dies on "no token for 'language-abc'".
- **⚠️ After EVERY upload/swap, verify the public+scheduled list** (`search.list forMine` + `videos.list status`). Swaps repeatedly left **stale public videos** and a **duplicate** in a slot — always re-list and reconcile. **This is now automated: `scripts/verify_uploads.py --channel <key> [--expected slots.json]`** — exits non-zero on any discrepancy, so make it the mandatory last step of every upload job. It detects duplicate/near-duplicate titles, stale `🗑️ DELETE —` videos still public, double-booked days, and (with `--expected`) empty slots + orphan videos.
- **A public video has NO `status.publishAt`** — only `snippet.publishedAt`. Match calendar slots on `publishAt or publishedAt`, or every already-published slot reads as EMPTY and every published video as an orphan.
- **`videos.update` REPLACES the whole part you send.** Sending `part=status` without `publishAt` **unschedules** the video; without `privacyStatus` it can re-privatise it. Always echo the current status back field-for-field, then **re-read and compare** — the playbook's "never unschedule via automation" rule is one missing key away from being broken by accident.
- **Never hard-delete via automation** (safety). To retire: set `private`. To hand the user an easy cleanup: **prefix titles `🗑️ DELETE — `** (rename ≠ delete) so they cluster in Studio for a bulk delete. Guard the keep-set by **ID + scheduled/public status** so a live/scheduled video can never be swept in.
- **`scripts/yt_cleanup.py --channel <key> [--marker '🗑️ DELETE'] [--apply]` closes the loop** on those marks so retirement never waits on a manual Studio session: dry-run by default (id / title / views / privacyStatus, exit 0, zero writes), `--apply` sets `privacyStatus=private` — **privatize, never delete** — and appends `channels/<key>/cleanup_log.json` `{video_id, title, old_status, new_status, ts}` so every change is auditable and undoable. Exits non-zero when a marked video is *not* retired, so `daily_check` surfaces it. Idempotent: already-private marks are a no-op, so it's safe on a cron.
  - **Selection is deliberately dumb, and that is the safety property:** a literal substring match on the operator's marker, nothing else. No fuzzy match, no "(superseded)" heuristics — *marking is a human act*, the script is only the janitor. A tool that infers what to retire will eventually infer wrong on a live video.
  - **A marked video that is still SCHEDULED is refused, not privatized** (needs explicit `--unschedule`): privatizing while leaving `publishAt` lets it auto-publish anyway, and clearing `publishAt` is an unschedule — which is never implicit. The cleared date goes into the log as `old_publish_at`, or "reversible" only gets you back to private-with-no-date.
- 🔴 **`videos.list` is read-after-write STALE** (measured 6 Aug 2026 on `4EgwUVBN7Ng`: a privacy update was acked, the very next `videos.list` still returned the OLD value, a later one was correct). The authority for "did the write land" is the **`videos.update` response**, which echoes the stored resource; the re-read is a *confirmation with a short retry*, not the verdict. Treating the first stale read as failure reports FAILED on a write that actually succeeded — and any daily check built on it cries wolf every run.
- **Read the whole audit log BEFORE opening it for write.** `open(p, "w")` truncates, so a `load()` inside the write block silently ate the history (caught on the first live `--apply`). And a crash in the *bookkeeping* step after a successful API call reads as "the action failed" — the same class of bug as the thumbnail-403 duplicate upload: log failures are warnings that print the record, never exceptions.
- **Numbering:** internal build-order ≠ publish-order; viewers never see numbers, so **don't rename asset folders** to "fix" it — just keep the publish schedule clean.
- Custom thumbnails need one-time **phone verification** on the account (kids channels used it; **claude-tricks NOT verified as of 5 Aug 2026** — thumbnails.set 403s until the human does youtube.com/features on that account).
- **Publish preflight (v12, 5 Aug 2026): every armed post is checked before upload** for (a) slot collisions — anything else publishing within ±6h on that channel — and (b) duplicate titles (normalized similarity ≥0.90), against BOTH `factory_posts` and the channel's **live YouTube uploads list** (the live check is what catches orphan uploads no post row tracks). Violations bounce the post back to `draft` with the warnings on the card (`post_preflight_blocked` event); re-arming after review is the deliberate override. Drafts also get a non-blocking heads-up at creation. Limitation: a *retitled* re-ship won't match by title — the human review of the draft card is still the catch for that.
- 🔴 **The video id in yt_upload output is the truth, not the exit code** (5 Aug 2026: a thumbnail 403 AFTER a successful upload marked the post FAILED "no video id" — and an earlier same-class failure got retried into a real DUPLICATE upload, two identical scheduled Shorts). Fixes now in place: `yt_upload.py` post-upload steps (thumbnail/playlist) are non-fatal warnings and it grew `--video-id` recovery mode (set extras on an existing video, no re-upload); `publish_post` schedules the post whenever an id is present in the output, recording the step failure as a warning. **Before ever re-arming a "failed" upload, check the channel's uploads list for the title first** — an instant-fail right after the auth line (no UPLOADED line) is the safe-to-retry signature; it's likely the daily upload limit (~3-4/day on a fresh channel), so retry next day.

When the verdict is 3, the only fix is a human `--setup-profile`; there is no clever recorder flag that logs in for you.

- **Fail → fix → proof belongs in ONE session — `record_demo.py --followup` (built Ep26, 2026-08-05).** For any "painful problem → one-move fix" short, cutting between two separate recordings is a *claim*; one continuous tape where the weak output, the added line and the fixed output share a conversation is a *proof* a viewer cannot dismiss. `--followup "<second prompt>"` types into the same chat after the first answer settles, and both halves get measured gates in the same spirit as `--seed-terms`: **`--fail-terms`** asserts the tell the episode is about really is in the first answer (≥1 match; every match recorded in `observed.fail_matched`) and **`--fix-forbid-terms`** asserts it is gone from the rewrite. Sidecar switches to the multi-punch form automatically (compose → weak answer → the one line → the rewrite), and the recorder sleeps `SETTLE_BEFORE_FOLLOWUP` then asserts the emitted windows against style_punch's own ramps so the style pass can never reject a take the recorder accepted. **Watch the comma:** the terms list splits on `,`, so a regex containing `{0,10}` silently becomes two terms — use alternations without braces.
- **A fix that ADDS context needs a POSITIVE gate — `record_demo.py --fix-seed-terms` (built #30, 2026-08-06).** `--fix-forbid-terms` proves a fix that *removes* a tell (Ep26's robotic phrases). The other half of the format — "you described your document; give it the document" — has no absence to measure: the proof is wording in the second answer that could only have come from the material the viewer just watched land. So the follow-up answer gets the same treatment as `--seed-terms`, and **the Ep24 lexical-never-numeric rule is what makes it real**: #30 gated on `Reinstatement Levy` / `Schedule C`, invented terms from its own pasted clause, because the *hedged* first answer had already emitted a plausible "1–2 months' rent" — any numeric gate would have passed on the answer the episode exists to reject. Sidecar gains `fix_seeded` / `fix_seeded_delay_s` / `observed.fix_seed_matched` (measured 0.21s after first token).
- **`--followup-paste FILE` is how the material lands, and on a no-login preset it is the ONLY way (#30).** It inserts the file's text in one motion and then human-types the follow-up after it, so the filmed order is the honest one (the page arrives, then the question) instead of 50 seconds of a legal clause being typed a character at a time. It is not a stylistic shortcut: **chatgpt.com's logged-out file inputs are all image-only** — measured 2026-08-06, all three `input[type=file]` elements carry `accept="image/*"` and a `.txt` returns *"Unable to upload"* — so an anonymous session can never attach a document. Note the page's own copy ("Log in to … upload files") is a *claim*; the three-input probe is the measurement, and running it is what turned the brief's guess into a fact before any VO was rendered.
- 🔴 **A payoff can fail by being off-SCREEN rather than late — that is a different bug from "don't scroll to hunt for it" (#30, 2026-08-06).** §10b bans scrolling to find a payoff that arrived late in the *text*, because a payoff beat has to play. #30 hit the other case: the seed gate passed at **+0.21s** — the grounded answer was immediate — but the pasted clause rendered ~1100px tall, so the answer streamed **below the fold**, and chatgpt.com mobile web does **not** auto-scroll to a streaming answer. The money line was never on camera, and no in-point could reach it. Fix belongs in the recorder: `--followup-scroll` scrolls to the **bottom of the conversation** (deterministic — the end of the answer, never a guessed pixel offset; it polls `scrollTop` and stops when it stops moving), then holds still for `PROOF_HOLD` and stamps `fix_scrolls` + a `fix_proof_start/end` window for the beat to cut to. Two consequences worth planning for: the beat that uses it should open *during* the last of the scroll so the move reads as intentional rather than as a jump, and it should carry **no punch camera** — a push on top of a scroll is two moves fighting.
- 🔴 **`probe_frames.py ink --thresh` is part of the measurement, not a default — and the region you probe is not the region you must verify (#30).** At the default `--thresh 34` a chat composer's own rounded **box fill** counts as ink against a black page, so #30's typing beat measured a 1.042 cap — no camera at all. At `--thresh 80` only the typed text counts and the honest cap is **1.114**. The converse bit on the same episode: the paste beat's cap was measured on the composer *alone* (1.084) and the crop then sliced the first character off every line of the answer still on screen above it ("he answer depends…"), so it had to come back to the whole-frame cap 1.042. **Isolate the content to find the cap, then re-measure the whole frame before shipping it** — `style_punch` crops the frame, not the region you probed.
- **Push the punch center to `style_punch`'s own clamp ceiling (`1 − 0.5/zoom`) when persistent chrome sits at a frame edge (#30).** A logged-out chat carries "Log in / Sign up for free" pills across the top; at the ink-capped zoom they were being *sliced* at the frame edge — §10c's "sliced white blob" — while fully cropping them needed more zoom than the text allowed. Moving the center to the clamp ceiling (0.550 at zoom 1.114) put the crop top at y197 and removed them outright, at zero cost because the content sat far below. The clamp is usually treated as a limit to avoid; it is also a free lever.
- **Element boxes are a floor for punch zoom, not the answer (Ep26).** `--followup`'s sidecar derives punch zooms from DOM bounding boxes, which on a mobile chat UI are full-width — it emitted 1.02–1.06 (i.e. barely a camera). Re-probing the shipped clip with `probe_frames.py ink --region <the pixels that must survive>` bought back 1.11 / 1.045 / 1.06 / 1.05. App chrome sharing the box (a "+" icon, a nav pill, a footer disclaimer) is **not** content worth protecting — exclude it via `--region`, or every window honestly reports `zoom_cap` 1.00 and the camera dies.
- **`scripts/record_memory_demo.py`** — sibling of `record_demo.py` for **settings/UI flows** rather than prompt→answer: same launch/context/`human_type` helpers and the same `t0`/`mark()` timeline convention, but it discovers the click path from on-screen labels, flips a switch, seeds a field and saves → `.timeline.json` with `toggle_click` / `seed_typing_start` / `seed_typing_end` / `save_click` plus a `punches` block for `style_punch.py`.
  - **Never hardcode a vendor click path** — ChatGPT and Claude both currently ship **two live layouts** mid-rollout (new "Memory summary" vs legacy "Reference saved memories"), and mobile web words it differently again. Try a ranked label set, record which label actually matched into `observed`, and abort printing the real on-screen text when none do. The recorded label is what the VO must then say.
  - **A toggle demo must film a state CHANGE.** Read `aria-checked`/`data-state` *before* clicking; if it is already ON, abort — a no-op click is not the proof beat. Re-read after the click and abort if it did not latch. Never film a switch that does not move.
  - **Redact PII in place, never edit the vendor UI.** The documented path (profile icon → Settings) runs through the account email; blur matching text nodes (`filter: blur(7px)`) before opening the menu and count the redactions into the sidecar. Blurring PII is fine; relabelling or mocking a vendor screen is not.
  - **Enforce beat separation in the recorder, not in the edit.** When one clip feeds two beats at different in-points, sleep to guarantee the gap (≥1.5s used here) and assert it against the final trimmed times before writing the sidecar.
- 🔴 **`answer_start` in the sidecar is NOT first-token — it equals `submit`.** The two were stamped on consecutive lines with nothing in between, so any beat in-point derived from `answer_start` opens on a spinner. `record_demo.py` now **polls the answer container while it films** and writes a measured `first_token` (Ep24: submit 15.45s → first_token 17.53s, i.e. a real ~2.1s streaming latency that `answer_start` hid). Anchor in-points to `first_token`; `answer_start` is retained only for back-compat.
- 🔴 **chatgpt.com mobile web ≠ desktop DOM.** At 540×960 with `MOBILE_UA` the app is the `wm-app` build: the answer is `li[data-message-role="assistant"] [data-assistant-markdown]`. The universally-quoted desktop selector `[data-message-author-role="assistant"]` matches **nothing** there — a check written against it doesn't error, it just reports "no answer ever rendered" forever. `record_demo.py:ANSWER_SELECTORS` tries mobile first, then desktop, and records which one matched into `observed.answer_selector`. (Same family of trap as lesson 2 below: on mobile web Enter inserts a newline and only the send button submits — verified again Ep24.)
- **"The answer visibly uses my context" must be a measured gate, not an eyeball.** `--seed-terms 'channel,beginner,45[- ]?min,short and concrete'` + `--personalized-within 3.0` makes `record_demo.py` exit non-zero unless seeded wording appears within N seconds of the first token, writing `first_personalized` / `personalized_delay_s` to the sidecar. The footage is kept on failure so a bad take can be inspected. The fix for a fail is a re-record (reseed / rephrase the prompt), **never** a scroll to hunt for the sentence — a payoff beat has to play.
- **Kill stray Chromium before re-running.** A crashed/killed run leaves `SingletonLock` in `.browser-profiles/<site>/` and the next launch dies with "profile is already in use". `pgrep -f "user-data-dir=.*browser-profiles/<site>"` → kill; the lock clears itself.
- 🔴 **Google SSO is blocked inside Chrome-for-Testing — do profile logins in REAL Chrome** (Ep24, 2026-08-05, cost one wasted login round). "Continue with Google" silently dead-ends in the automation build (`g_state` cookie written, no session ever minted). Launch the headed login with `channel="chrome"` on the SAME profile dir (Playwright uses its mock keychain for both builds, so cookies stay readable by the chromium recorder), or use ChatGPT's email login which works in any build. **Verify the login by cookie, not by "window closed":** the session only exists once `__Secure-next-auth.session-token` (chunked `.0/.1`) is in `Default/Cookies` — a half-finished handoff leaves a full `auth.openai.com` session and NO chatgpt.com token (user closed the window during the final redirect; clicking "Log in" again completes it silently, no credentials).
- 🔴 **Logged-in chatgpt.com keeps HIDDEN duplicates of key controls — filter every locator with `visible=true`** (Ep24, measured three times). The sidebar button has *no* aria-label (only `data-testid='open-sidebar-button'`), `accounts-profile-button` exists twice, and the composer keeps a hidden fallback `<textarea name="prompt-textarea">` while the real editor is a different element — a bare `.first` resolves the hidden copy and times out 30s later. Logged-in mobile click path (measured): header has NO profile control → sidebar → account row at sidebar bottom → profile menu → **direct "Personalization" item** (no Settings hop). The sheet autosaves but a real Save button appears once a field is edited.
- 🔴 **Arm PII blur BEFORE the click that reveals the PII — a post-click sweep loses the race** (Ep24, cost two takes). The profile-menu header paints the account name ~1s before any sweep that runs after the click. Capture the name from the sidebar row *while it is visible* (it reads empty once the menu covers it), install a MutationObserver that blurs matching text the instant it mounts (microtask = before paint), then assert zero exposed nodes and frame-extract to verify. CSS-injected blur (`a[href^='/c/']`, the account row) covers the virtualized chat list including rows that mount later.
- 🔴 **DOM state is not pixels: the Enable-memory switch renders a SPINNER long past `aria-checked=true`** (Ep24, cost two takes). The click flips aria-checked instantly, Playwright counts the spinner as a "visible switch", and the blue ON pill does NOT render in place in filmable time — it appears after Save triggers a refetch. Structure the take as flip (hold the honest spinner) → seed → Save → **return to the row and pixel-gate the proof shot** (blue-fraction of the row screenshot > 0.006; the ON pill is its only saturated blue, ≈1.2% of row pixels), reopening the sheet on camera as fallback. Bonus: the Save also pops a green "Custom instructions updated" toast — vendor-confirmed save, on camera. Poster = the proof frame.
- 🔴 **Memory ON leaks the operator's REAL life into the answer** (Ep24 — a take put private project names on camera and was scrapped). With Enable memory on, old saved memories personalize the re-ask with actual work history — unshippable PII even though the gate passes. For a filmable personalized answer: seed in **custom instructions** with Enable memory **OFF** — custom instructions personalize regardless of the toggle, so the answer derives from exactly the line the viewer watched being typed and nothing else. Expect generic rolls: the model may ignore the seed entirely (one take opened pure productivity boilerplate); retry until the gate passes rather than loosening it.
- 🔴 **Acceptance regexes must be lexical, never numeric** (Ep24). `45[- ]?min` was gamed by a generic "30–45 minutes" range in an answer with zero real personalization. Gate on words that can ONLY come from the seed (`channel,beginner,YouTube`) and read `observed.answer_head` before trusting any pass.
- **Dark theme = `color_scheme="dark"` on the context** (`--dark` in `record_demo.py`, always-on in `record_memory_demo.py`) — the account's Appearance stays "System", nothing to reset. Note the continuity cost: clips recorded before the switch (demo_cold) are light-theme.
- **Lessons:** (1) headless-shell gets bot-blocked — use full Chromium `channel="chromium"` + `--disable-blink-features=AutomationControlled`; (2) Enter often inserts a newline instead of submitting — every site needs a submit-button selector; (3) auto-dismiss popups before typing, ~2s after submit, AND before the scroll pass (late banners); (4) clear the input first (Cmd+A/Backspace) — profiles persist drafts and you get a doubled prompt; (5) record 540x960 mobile-UA viewport, lanczos-upscale 2x, crf 18.

## 10c. Pro framing styles (fixes "cropped/not-pro" raw recordings — NEVER center-crop)
- **`scripts/style_punch.py IN OUT [--timeline TL.json]`** — default for native 9:16: eased punch-zoom ~1.30x into the input box during typing (anchored at the text's extent so nothing readable is cut), ease-out on submit, slow 1.08x push during the answer; auto-detects moments via frame-differencing when no timeline. Renders via rawvideo→PIL subpixel crop (smoother than zoompan at slow speeds).
  - **Measure the ink, then set the zoom caps — don't trust the defaults** (Ep24). Mobile chat answers run **edge-to-edge** (measured x 33→1038 of 1080), so the default `Z_PUSH` 1.08 clips the outermost characters; max safe = `1080/ink_span` = 1.074. Sidecar now accepts **`push_zoom`** alongside `type_zoom` — set it from a real frame (used 1.04). Same for typing: anchoring on the prompt-text→send-button span gave `type_zoom` 1.20 + `input_center` [0.542, 0.53], sacrificing only the far-left "+" icon.
  - 🔴 **The typing punch is held past submit, so its window must include the STREAMING PIP, not just the composer** (Ep24 v2). Composer ink measured x 64→1031 (cap 1.085), but the moment you hit send ChatGPT paints its pending-answer dot at the answer column's left edge, **x 33** — further left than anything in the composer — and the held punch cropped it in half for ~1s. Anchor on the *union* (33→1031, cap 1.061). Costs ~0.02 of zoom; the alternative is a sliced white blob riding the frame edge.
  - **Sweep the WHOLE clip, not just the answer window** — that pip lands during the submit hold, which an answer-window-only sweep never looks at. Decode the styled output at 2 *and* 5 fps and take the min edge margin over every frame (this take: 10px, 143 frames).
  - **Multi-punch sidecar for UI-flow footage** (Ep24): settings recordings have no submit/answer arc for the default camera to hang on. Pass `"punches": [{t0, t1, center, zoom, label}, …]` instead and `PunchCamera` sits at full frame, eases onto each moment (`PUNCH_IN` 0.55s), holds, eases back (`PUNCH_OUT` 0.65s). Overlapping windows *including their ramps* are a **hard error** — fix the spacing in the recording, don't blend two beats into one move. Sidecars without `punches` take the original path unchanged.
  - 🔴 **The recorder's beat-gap constant must equal the punch-window+ramp math, one number, both ends** (Ep24 — a clean take was rejected by the style pass because the recorder enforced 1.5s while the windows it emitted need ≥2.95s with ramps). `record_memory_demo.py` now sleeps AND asserts against the exact windows its sidecar emits.
  - **Verify clipping by sweep, not by eyeballing 2 frames:** decode the styled output at fps=2 and assert the darkest-pixel column extent never touches the frame edge across the whole answer window (Ep24 min margin: 20px both sides). A spot-check misses the peak of a slow push.
- **`scripts/style_device.py IN OUT [--accent HEX]`** — phone-in-frame on dark premium gradient + accent glow (default #E91E63), rounded corners, soft shadow, 1.00→1.05 drift; layout solver keeps caption-safe top 200/bottom 300 clear at max zoom (chips never cover content).
- **`scripts/style_blurbg.py IN OUT`** — desktop/odd-aspect footage: blurred-darkened fill bg + full recording sharp with 48px margins + auto-capped punch (zoom reduced until zero content crop). minterpolate REJECTED — it ghosts UI text; use fps=30 frame-hold.
- **Craft rules learned:** supersample 2x before any ffmpeg zoompan (kills jitter); on ffmpeg 8.x `overlay=shortest=1` with `-loop 1` images never terminates — cap with `-frames:v`; full-res noise dither becomes temporal grain under zoom (blows up bitrate) — low-frequency only.
- **Two ffmpeg traps that both cost a rebuild on Ep25 v3 (2026-08-05):**
  - 🔴 **`crop`'s `w`/`h` are evaluated ONCE at filter-config time — only `x`/`y` are per-frame.** So the playbook's own "animated crop, not zoompan" Ken Burns recipe (§15) can only *translate*; a `t` expression on the width/height hard-errors (`Error when evaluating the expression`). For a true push-in, `zoompan` over a 2x-supersampled source is the supported path, and §15 already carves out exactly that exception.
  - 🔴 **`-loop 1 -t <dur> -i still.png` produces 25 fps, not your output rate.** A 5.12s hold silently arrives as 128 frames instead of 154; feed it into an `overlay` and the pane just *freezes* for the last 0.7s while the other input keeps playing, which looks like a stutter bug rather than a frame-count bug. Always pass `-framerate <fps>` on a looped-still input, and probe `nb_frames` against `round(dur*fps)` before trusting the clip.

## 13. Content strategy patterns that worked
- **Beginner-first for reach:** teach people using AI at 10% of its potential (model switching, thinking/effort, memory, projects, connectors) — 100× the audience of dev-only tips.
- **News-forward mix** (a channel can be daily when ~45% is same-day news): e.g. AI Unpacked = ~35% product/efficiency tips + ~20% brand-new capabilities + ~45% latest news. News rows **never pre-written**.
- **Series that bind:** each short is a standalone *and* a chapter of a long-form compilation.
- **Command hooks + stakes** ("Stop letting X…") with the weak result already on screen in frame 1 (≤1.5s).
- 🔴 **Retention is a SUSTAIN problem, not a hook problem — and it's now MEASURED, not guessed (`scripts/yt_retention.py`, 2026-08-06).** We were tuning the 3-second hook against a single `avg_view_pct`. The real per-video curve (YouTube Analytics `elapsedVideoTimeRatio` → `audienceWatchRatio`) settles it on the two claude-tricks episodes with the widest avg-view gap: **Ep1 `Y8MdxiUgkbo` (48.2% avg) and Ep2 `pSpkEbSiTHU` (70.8% avg) have essentially IDENTICAL 3s holds (95% vs 103%) — the ENTIRE gap is the 15s sustain (53% vs 80%).** The hook is not where these are won or lost; the 3–15s stretch is. Rule for every Short: budget the **0–15s window** the way we used to budget the hook — a face/payoff early, no dead beat, one clear idea landing before 15s. After ~48h run `python scripts/yt_retention.py --channel KEY --summary` and read the **15s-hold** column, not the average. Two corollaries this window forces:
  - **No dense, dark, techy beat inside the 0–15s sustain window for a beginner audience.** Ep25 v2 (the cut that actually shipped) put a black terminal (`claude -p … [thinking…]`) at ~13–15s — exactly the average drop point — and the payoff host face didn't arrive until ~21s (≈60% already gone). A real-terminal VHS tape is a proof asset, but it belongs AFTER the payoff or capped to a <1.5s texture flash; never as the beat under a key line while the viewer is still deciding. Front-load the human, backload the density.
  - **Board/dashboard footage must show THIS episode's own tiles, or the opening is a narrate-what-you-see lie (§1.4).** Ep25 v2 opened on the review board while it was rendering a DIFFERENT episode ("Your AI Forgets You") for ~4s under the VO "this video was … made by a factory." `beat_map §4` predicted the fallback and flagged it as a *warn*; it shipped anyway. The receipt on screen must be the video you're claiming it made — treat it as a hard gate at record time, not a warning.
- **Opening pacing — the intro overlay is a BEAT, not a flash (operator feedback, teaser Ep25, 5 Aug 2026).** The measured failure: the title overlay was effectively gone by ~1s, and step chips (PLANNED→RECORDED) flew past at ~1s each, all composited over busy B-roll. The locked structure for every Short: **(1) intro/title overlay HOLDS 2.5–4s** — one readable beat, VO hook playing under it, overlay in dead space or over a calm frame, never over busy motion; **(2) then the HOST on camera** delivers the setup line full-frame; **(3) then the B-roll run** — screen recordings alternating with host cutaways, mix-n-match cuts and split-screen (host + screen) where a beat needs both; each overlay chip that carries information holds ≥1.5s. Assembly must derive the overlay hold from the VO beat it belongs to, not from a fixed dissolve default.
  - 🔴 **Retro-fitting that structure onto an already-approved asset set has ONE hard limit: a lip-synced host clip can only ever carry the lines it was rendered for (Ep25 v3, 2026-08-05).** Ep25's host asset was 6.20s of HeyGen for lines 7–8 and beat 7 ate 6.153s of it, so there was no host motion left for the mandated on-camera setup line. Playing the lip-synced motion under a *different* line is fake lip-sync, which §6 bans — so the host beat becomes a **frozen mouth-closed frame with a slow push** (`scripts/make_split_beat.py`, `scripts/make_still_hold.py`), and full-frame gives way to a **split** (screen top / host bottom, geometry mirrored from `Short.tsx`'s newsSplit so it reads as the channel's existing grammar, not a new one). Pick the freeze frame from a **mouth-region darkness sweep**, not by eye. When the setup line *points at something* ("that's the real board"), the split is not a compromise — a full-frame host would break narrate-what-you-see (§1.4) on the one line that needs both.
  - **Under the held title, freeze the frame — a still is the only guaranteed-calm frame.** Ep25 v2 ran the poster over a scrolling board; v3 held it over the episode's own `hook_board_still` baked into a 5.12s clip at 1.000→1.030, which also bought 1.7s of *readable* receipt after the dissolve. The still-frame asset most of these episodes already produce for the hook is exactly this beat's material and is otherwise never shipped.
  - **Budget the cost before pinning the hold: `Short.tsx` draws `Cover` ABOVE the captions**, so a 3.4s hold hides the hook's first ~3 hot words. Accept it when the poster states the same claim in type (two text systems fighting one beat reads worse), but know it is a trade, not free. `cover.until` is an episode-level override in `build_ep_v2.py` — the derived default is `hook_end + 0.6`, i.e. **0.60s on any episode with no `host` beats**, which is what "gone by ~1s" actually was.
  - **A transparent-PNG end card has no home in the spec** (`ShortProps` has no overlay prop; `kind:"image"` replaces a whole beat) — so it gets hand-composited with ffmpeg after the fact and then **silently goes missing on the next re-cut**. Ep25 v2 shipped it that way; v3 made it a `cfg["endcard"] = {src, in_s, out_s}` step in `build_ep_v2.py`, run between the master and the outro concat so it can never bleed onto the sting, windowed from the asset's own `endcard.json`.
  - **One line can serve both the host beat and narrate-what-you-see — split the LINE, not just the frame (Ep25 v4, 2026-08-05).** When the setup line both needs the host on camera *and* points at the screen ("That's the real board. **Every green tile** is a piece of this Short."), the choice is not full-frame-vs-split: hold the host **full-frame for the first sentence**, then cut to the split at the **measured word onset** of the thing the VO names (`make_split_beat.py --host-live --lead-full <onset − line_start>`; 1.312s here, from `vo_*.words.json`). The host owns the sentence that points; the board is on screen before the words "green tile". This retires the v3 compromise — with a clip lip-synced to *that* line, the split's host pane carries **real motion** (`--host-live`), and the frozen-mouth degrade stays only on beats no clip was ever rendered for.
  - **The split's screen pane is a 1050px window, and the top of the page is not always where the proof is (Ep25 v4).** `make_split_beat --screen-y` slides it. Ep25's green filmstrip tiles measure **y 952–1056** with labels to ~1140, so a top-aligned pane sliced them at the divider; `--screen-y 120` (window 120–1170) holds the episode title **and** the whole tile row — the two things the line actually names. Measure the ink rows (green-channel dominance for status chips, bright-row bands for headings), don't eyeball the pane.
  - 🔴 **A comment-KEYWORD end card must survive the outro sting, or the last frame contradicts the CTA (Ep25 v4).** v3 put the FACTORY card up for 2.38s and then handed the final **3.8s** — the frames a viewer decides on — to the reusable sting's generic *"GOT FEEDBACK? COMMENT 💬"* chip. The keyword the whole teaser is *measured* on was gone before the video ended, and the reviewer read the cut as "a generic COMMENT card" even though the FACTORY card had rendered correctly. Fix, in the builder so it cannot go missing on the next re-cut: `endcard["over_outro"]=True` composites the card **after** the concat, from its in-point to the last frame, and `cfg["outro_dur"]` trims the sting **before its own comment chip appears** (measured off the sting's animation: avatar ~0.6s, SUBSCRIBE ~0.8s, generic COMMENT chip ~1.65s) — two comment CTAs on one frame is the same defect wearing a hat. Trimming is a *cut*, never a re-render of the shared branding asset (§15), and the bed's fade-out must be re-derived from the trimmed length (a fade pinned at 2.6s never fires inside a 1.6s sting). Cost to know: a ≤4s tail cap buys the keyword at the price of the sting's own hold (SUBSCRIBE readable 0.80s here, under the ≥1.5s chip bar) — the keyword wins, but write the number down rather than pretending the trade is free.
  - **Re-cuts need their own filenames or they eat the original.** `build_ep_v2.py --tag v3` renames every render *and the props JSON* (`episodes/<ep>.<tag>.json`); before that the spec path was hardcoded to `.v2.json` and a re-cut clobbered the shipped spec — unrecoverable, since this repo is not under git.
  - 🔴 **The craft fixes are worthless if the wrong cut ships — verify the scheduled draft is the LATEST approved render before it goes live (AI Unpacked Ep25, 2026-08-06).** Every host-early / endcard / lip-sync fix above (v3, v4) landed on disk and NONE reached viewers. The upload log shows `qS-VzOsMFiA` was armed from `ep25_v2_outro.mp4` — the FIRST cut, scheduled 10:30 — at 13:34; then v3 (15:47) and v4 (17:02, runtime trimmed to 29.5s) were rebuilt to fix exactly the retention faults this page documents, and the manifest note "remove the old scheduled video before arming the v3 draft" **never executed**, so the audience got v1. A published Short cannot have its file swapped, so the loss is permanent for that video, and the analytics that trigger the next round of "fixes" are measuring the cut we already know is worst. Gate: before a scheduled draft's publish time, assert its armed render is the newest `approved` cut for that episode (latest `renders/<ep>_*` approved assembly vs the calendar's armed render id); if a newer approved cut exists, re-arm or cancel the schedule. A rebuild that never swaps in is a silent regression, not an improvement.
- **The LYRIC DUEL — a comment format that is also a funnel (Aashiqana, 2026-08-06).** A derivative cut that pits two lines of an already-shipped song against each other: frame-zero VERSUS card → line 1 over one still with a "1" chip → **hard cut on the next line's measured vocal attack** → line 2 over a different still with the "2" chip in the opposite corner → recap card with both lines stacked → announce card. Why it works as an instrument: *"1 ya 2?" is a far lower-effort ask than "which song next?"* — the viewer already has both options on screen and only has to type a digit, so it is the cheapest real engagement signal a music channel can buy, and `yt_engage.py --count-keyword` can read the result. Three rules it needs: (1) the two beats must be **different stills**, or the duel is a caption change over one shot; (2) the numeral chips are the format's whole grammar, so they follow the §4 dead-space rule and sit in **opposite** corners — probe both against the real rendered beats, not the source stills; (3) the closing card must point at something that is **live now**, because the duel's second job is the funnel.
- 🔴 **An announce card states a fact about the world on publish day — re-check it against what is actually live, not against the plan (Aashiqana lyric duel revision, 2026-08-06).** The first scope of this cut ended on *"Naya gaana AAJA VE — Friday"*. Aaja Ve had been **live since Aug 3**: the card would have told viewers to wait for a song they could already play, which is worse than no card — it converts a funnel into a reason to leave. The card became "AAJA VE — ab live 🥀 / Channel pe suno." Generalize it: any card containing a **date, a tense, or the word "coming"** is a claim with an expiry, and the gate is the channel's live uploads list plus `BRAND-BIBLE.md`'s song log, checked at *render* time. This is §1.5 ("verify reality at production time") applied to the one frame a viewer acts on.
- **In-house data-mock for pricing/leaderboard news (AI Unpacked Ep23, 2026-08-05):** never screenshot a real leaderboard/pricing site or show a trademarked logo — an own generic "index" graphic with an "ILLUSTRATIVE" footer reads as a real news-graphic at zero compliance risk. **Rendering it, however, goes through the locked `StatBars` Remotion component (§4), never a pre-baked image** — Ep23's PIL card (`gen_ep23_pricebars.py`) got cover-cropped in the 9:16 pane (labels cut off both edges). Keep the compliance lesson, retire the PIL layout.
- **In-house mock of a *chat UI* — the grammar that keeps it honest (Ep24, 2026-08-05, `scripts/make_chat_mock.py`).** Same rule as the data-mock above: never mock a real vendor's screen as if it were a capture. Neutral dark chat, generic "AI ASSISTANT" label, **no vendor name / logo / lookalike colour**, our own magenta send button, ILLUSTRATIVE footer. Two craft points that make it read at 1.5s: (1) **colour is the argument** — put a yellow marker under the fact the user states and render the AI's redundant question in the *same* yellow; the eye links them with the sound off, no arrows or callout chips needed. (2) **The disclosure goes INSIDE the caption-safe band** (here y≈1245), not in the bottom-300 dead zone where an overlay chip would sit on it — the whole point of a disclosure is that it can't be covered. Unlike the retired Ep23 PIL card, a PIL still is fine when it's authored at native 1080x1920 and never re-panned (the Ep23 failure was cover-crop into a 9:16 pane, not PIL itself).
- **In-house mock of a *document-grounding* surface — `scripts/make_doc_mock.py` (AI Unpacked #27, 2026-08-06).** Third member of the mock family (data → `StatBars`, chat → `make_chat_mock.py`, documents → this), for any beat about "give an AI your files". Four states at native 1080×1920 — `sources` (files landed), `answer` (grounded answer with citation chips), `compute` (a generic code cell + RUN), `rollout` (who has the feature) — same house grammar: no vendor name/logo/lookalike colour, ILLUSTRATIVE footer, channel palette. Three craft points that transfer: (1) **centre the body in the safe band, don't pin a start-y** — each state has a different block height, and a fixed start left one balanced and the next hugging the top with 900px of dead frame; draw the body on a transparent layer, take its alpha bbox, centre it, and **assert it ends above y1420** so the caption band is structurally empty (a mock is authored, not captured — there is no excuse for content under the captions). (2) **A progressive `--reveal` beats a second still** — two consecutive beats on the same frame read as a freeze bug, and "files land → question asked" is a real state change for free. The result is honest, compliant, and *weaker on proof* than the episode before it, which shipped one unbroken real tape. Write that down in the ledger rather than letting a well-made mock quietly pass as a demo — and treat the fix as a scheduling item (a human `--setup-profile` login), not a design problem.
- **A measure pass that draws leaves ghost ink.** Getting a letterspaced string's width by "drawing it with alpha 0 and reading the pen x" put a full black headline at (0,0) — PIL's `ImageDraw(mode="RGBA")` on an **RGB** canvas ignores the alpha. - **Multi-entity compliance:** when a script names more than one AI lab (e.g. Anthropic + OpenAI + Google), the "Not affiliated with X" disclaimer must list every named lab, not just whichever one the channel's boilerplate already had.

  - **The "photo" inside a media mock must be DRAWN, never stock.** The tile is what the word *deepfake* ends up pointing at, and a real photographer's frame — or worse, a real person's face — under that word asserts something about that person. An abstract field asserts nothing. Same reasoning as the Ep28 b-roll catch, one step earlier in the pipeline.
  - **Naming a public body in plain type is attribution; its emblem is not ours to use.** A regulation is cited as "REGULATION (EU) 2024/1689 · ARTICLE 50" in type, and the "AI" badge glyph is deliberately generic so it can never be mistaken for the official Code-of-Practice icon set. A lockup would also imply endorsement, which is a second problem wearing the first one's coat.
- 🔴 **An overlay does not have to COVER content to be a §4 defect — printing the SAME STRING twice is the same failure at low volume (AI Unpacked #29, 2026-08-06).** Every chip corner scored **0.00** on both mock holds and every chip was legally placed; the rendered frames still showed the step chip reading `IN FORCE — NOT DEFERRED` sitting ~40px from the mock's own chrome header reading `IN FORCE — NOT DEFERRED`, and a second beat printing `TWO KINDS OF LABEL` three times (chip + chrome header + inner label). No corner probe, caption-band check or safe-band assert can catch this — they all measure *occlusion*. **QC every overlay against the words already on the frame, not just the pixels.** The fix belongs in the generator, not the episode: a mock's chrome headers are now chosen to be *distinct from the chip labels an episode is likely to put over them*, so every future beat inherits it (same doctrine as the `StatBars` baseline fix).
- **If the string is a variable, the chip is a MEASUREMENT (#29).** A status pill drawn to a hardcoded right edge ran its last letter through its own border the moment the label got long — on the pill naming the enforcement target, of all of them. `tracked_w()` + padding, always; this is the same lesson as `Cover`'s autoshrink and `hilite_line`'s `textbbox`, and it will keep recurring until every chip in the repo is sized from ink.
- **The bed-pre-gain sensitivity now has FIVE measurements and they disagree: 0.40 / 0.65 / 0.56 / 0.62 / 0.50 LU per dB** (playbook original, #27 at 39s, #28 at 30s, #29 at 37s, #30 at 30s). That is enough evidence to stop treating any of them as *the* figure: compute the first correction from the newest number, then re-measure and stop inside 0.3 LU of the reference. #29 went −19.2 → **−18.4**, an exact match to the shipped #26, in one bisect step.
- 🔴 **A stock b-roll clip can ARGUE AGAINST the line it plays under — QC the actor, not the mood (Ep28, caught at frame QC, cost one re-render).** The beat was the episode's *secondary hook*, "Nobody told it to." The clip was picked off a search for "nighttime software workspace" — right palette, right energy, and it shows **a human at a keyboard**, i.e. the frame says someone told it to. This is a narrate-what-you-see failure (§1.4) that neither the chip-corner probe nor the caption-band check can catch, because nothing is *covered* — the composition is clean and the meaning is inverted. **Before a b-roll beat ships, say the VO line out loud against a real extracted frame and ask what the frame asserts.** For any "the machine did this by itself" beat, the clip must contain **no person at all** (abstract network/data animation), which is also the cheapest thing to verify. Same pass caught the `|split` mode cropping the host pane through the mouth on that outfit's framing — the one place a lip-synced clip must never be cut. Both fixed by going `|full` with a person-free clip.

## 15. The apple-to-apple quality ratchet (all channels)
_Discovered on the Lulla audit (2026-08-02): a consistent channel can quietly **plateau** — every episode on-brand, none actually better than the last. The fix is a ratchet._

- **Each channel keeps a `QUALITY-LEDGER.md`** (`channels/<key>/QUALITY-LEDGER.md`). It records the current **bar** (the newest/best-produced episode + its exact params), a **scorecard** of quality axes, a ranked **delta queue**, and a **per-episode log** of *how each new episode beat the previous one*.
- **The gate:** a new episode must be **≥ the bar on every axis and strictly better on ≥1 weak axis**, with **no regression on the brand's spine** (its consistency/palette/pacing identity). Apple-to-apple = same channel, same character, same format — never compare across channels.
- **Anti-templating ceiling (Pipeline A):** no new episode may reuse **>50% of any prior episode's frames**. Reshuffling the same still library reads as mass-produced (July-2025 inauthentic-content rule) and is a *regression*, not a new episode. Budget ≥6–8 fresh on-model scenes per episode.
- **Bookends hygiene:** a designed intro/outro isn't *shipped* until it's rendered to `bookends/intro.mp4` + `outro.mp4` **and** wired into the publish path (`add_bookends.py`). Candidate stings sitting as loose mp3s = zero branded videos. Audit the publish script, not the design doc.
- **Why global:** every channel accumulates locked params and then coasts on them. The ledger turns "stay consistent" into "consistently improve," and its scorecard axes (freshness, sing-along/comprehension support, signature bookend, motion richness, format depth) transfer across Pipeline A and B.

**Reusable craft from the Lulla #6 build (2026-08-02):**
- **Time lyric/caption overlays to a sung track with `faster_whisper` (on-machine, free).** Transcribe the finished song → segment start/end times → but **display YOUR known-correct lyrics** at those anchors (whisper mishears sung words — "moon-pond"→"moon upon"). One `WhisperModel("base","cpu","int8")` pass over a 2.5-min song is seconds. This is how a Pipeline-A song gets accurate sing-along captions without hand-timing. Tool: `scripts/lulla_captions.py` (spec `{lines:[{text,start,end,pos,size,fade}]}`).
- **Calm-channel caption style ≠ Shorts caption style.** For bedtime/kids-calm, use **soft cream (`#FDF6E3`) rounded text, lower third, blurred drop-shadow, slow alpha fades** — NOT the Anton/magenta/spring-pop Shorts treatment (§4). Match the caption energy to the channel's energy.
- **Lyric↔visual sync is nearly free and reads premium:** order the shot list so the scene under a line matches it (the "plump the moss" line over the moss-pillow scene). Costs nothing, lifts perceived production value.
- **Bookend duration for calm channels:** ~8s intro / ~10s outro; end the episode body ON the ritual scene (drop a shot so the belly-dim/goodnight frame is last, not cut by `-shortest`).

**Reusable craft from the Lulla 1-hour compilation build (2026-08-06) — binding published episodes into a long-form sleep aid:**
- 🔴 **A "progressive" audio ramp that ends in a fixed-duration trim SILENTLY DELETES THE END OF EVERY SEGMENT.** `assemble_compilation.py`'s `dim_arc` applied `atempo` to each segment and then conformed the audio to that segment's **video** duration (`atrim=0:dur`) so the crossfade offsets could not drift. Both halves are individually correct and the combination is a content bug: `atempo=0.93` stretches the audio to ~1.077×, and the trim throws the overflow away — so the last ~7% of the *musical* content is never heard, growing with segment index. At the 14th segment (`atempo=0.916`) a 160s song loses about **15 seconds — its entire closing cadence** — and nothing in the output looks wrong, because the video is exactly the right length. Measured, don't reason: put a distinct tone in the last second of a test file, run it through `atempo,atrim`, and band-pass the tail. **Any time a filter changes duration upstream of a fixed trim, ask what got trimmed off.** Fix was to scope the tempo ramp (`dim_tempo_scope`, default `interlude`) — the brightness ramp is free of this because `eq` does not change duration.
- **When a compilation's premise is "each published episode appears once, unmodified", the grade is the only treatment that qualifies.** Brightness/vignette is a presentation layer; anything that alters timing, pitch or length is an edit, and an edit makes the compilation a *different cut* of the songs rather than a binder of them. Worth writing into the spec, because the tool will happily do both.
- 🔴 **A fixed per-segment step + a clamp is not a ramp — it is a ramp followed by a flat line, and the flat part lands exactly where the effect mattered most.** `dim_arc`'s brightness was `max(-0.20, -0.02*i)`, which hits the floor at segment **11 of 15**: the hour's final **28 minutes — the whole "sinking into the dark" coda — rendered at one identical brightness**, while the first half did all the dimming. Nothing errors and every frame is on-brand. **Derive the step from the actual segment count** (`floor / (n-1)`) so the last segment lands *on* the floor and every segment in between moves. Generalises to any "progressive" effect whose step was tuned on a shorter piece than the one you are now building — check the value at the LAST index, not just that the direction is right.
- **A per-join crossfade RAMP needs the running-timeline offset, not `i * xfade`.** With one flat crossfade `offset = sum(durs[:i]) - i*xfade` happens to be right; the moment joins differ it must be `sum(durs[:i]) - sum(xfades[:i])`. Getting this wrong drifts slowly and only shows up deep into a long build, so **make the tool print predicted vs actual duration and warn on divergence** — a 15-segment/60-minute graph is not something you eyeball.
- **Validate a long-form filtergraph at FULL SCALE with synthetic sources.** A 14-deep chained `xfade`/`acrossfade` graph over an hour has failure modes a 3-segment test does not, but you do not need the real assets to find them: `lavfi` `color` + `sine` segments at the **exact planned durations**, 320×180, `-preset ultrafast`, proves the duration arithmetic and the chain depth for a few minutes of CPU. Do this *before* spending generation credits on the real material.
- 🔴 **Check which render is actually the published one — `_breathe` / `_silent` intermediates have NO AUDIO STREAM.** Lulla's `CONTENT-CALENDAR.csv` pointed five of six shipped rows at `*_breathe.mp4`, which are the Ken-Burns video-only passes (`ffprobe` shows a lone video stream); the files that were uploaded are `songNN.mp4` / `song06_final.mp4`. A compilation built off the calendar's own asset column would have been **57 minutes of silence** and looked fine in every frame check. `ffprobe -select_streams a:0` every input before trusting a path, and treat a calendar/ledger asset column as a claim, not a fact.
- **Ordering a compilation: measure first, but expect the measurement's real finding to be "there is no arc".** `scripts/song_energy.py` (spectral-flux onset envelope → autocorrelation tempo, onset rate, spectral centroid, ebur128 LRA; no librosa needed) ranked six Lulla songs — and the ranking **completely reshuffled** when the tempo term was dropped, because autocorrelation tempo is octave-ambiguous (song02 read 68 BPM at r=0.433 against 136 BPM at r=0.411 — a coin flip). The honest conclusion for a tightly-branded calm channel is that its episodes are near-uniform by design, so use the measurement where it is *stable* (the extremes, and the octave-free measures like centroid) and let **lyric function** order the middle. Do not report a composite score as an arc when three of its four terms disagree.
- **Bookend arithmetic is per-tool: `add_bookends.py` CONCATENATES (+18.0s exactly), it does not crossfade.** Verified against a shipped file rather than assumed (`song06_final` 166.01 = `song06_lyrics` 148.00 + 8 + 10). Budgeting an hour to the second means measuring the bookend path, not inferring it from the crossfade discipline used everywhere else.
- **Leonardo: `num_images=3` is rejected at 1344×768 ("too many images requested") and a LOCKED SEED makes a 2-image batch near-duplicates.** For a sequence of distinct scenes, write one prompt per still at `num_images=1` — crossfading two variations of one composition reads as a freeze bug, which is the same defect §13 flags for two consecutive beats on one mock frame.

**Reusable craft from the Aashiqana #01 hook-cut Short (2026-08-05) — deriving a second Short from a shipped release, zero new generation:**
- **A "hook cut" is a distinct asset class from an episode. Log it as a derivative, never on the ledger.** It reuses 100% of a shipped release's shots by design, so scoring it against the anti-templating ceiling is a category error — the ceiling governs *new episodes*. The gate for a derivative is different and simpler: it must open on a **different bar of the song** and use a **different visual grammar** than the Short already live, or it is a duplicate upload.
- **Trust the alignment, not the old timing file.** Song #01's shipped `timing_final.json` put the last-chorus hook at 157.06s; re-running `align_karaoke.py` on the demucs stem put it at 154.95s, and a 20ms RMS scan found the actual downbeat transient at **155.28s** with the vocal attacking at 155.50s. A 1.8s error is invisible in a 3.5-min long-form and fatal in a Short that must *start* on the hook. **Re-align before every derivative cut; never inherit line times across a re-cut.**
- **Find the cold-open frame-0 with a 20ms RMS scan, not by ear or by the aligner.** `ffmpeg -af "aresample=8000,asetnsamples=160,astats=metadata=1:reset=1,ametadata=print:key=lavfi.astats.Overall.RMS_level:file=-"` over the candidate second prints a per-20ms level curve. Cut on the **percussive transient 0.2s before the vocal** (visible in both the mix and the `no_vocals` stem) — the Short then opens on a drum hit and the hook word lands immediately after, which reads deliberate instead of clipped.
- **volumedetect ranks candidates; meaning picks the winner.** The loudest 2s window in *Baarish Aur Tum* was a verse opener (−15.0 dB). The chosen hook was the second-loudest (−15.9 dB) because it is the **title line, and the two lines after it are the two most-devotional lines in the song** — cold-opening there buys 23s of unbroken payoff, where the verse opener buys a build. Rank objectively, then choose the candidate whose *next 20 seconds* are strongest.
- **The vertical "lyric band", not a full-bleed centre crop.** A 16:9 → 9:16 cover crop keeps only **~32% of the source width** and decapitates every two-shot. Instead: 1080×1200 band at y=250 (a 0.9-aspect crop = **~51% of width survives**), over a cover-scaled, `boxblur=40:2`, `eq=brightness=-0.20:saturation=0.72` version of the same frame, with a 2px `#F0C45C@0.30` hairline top and bottom so the band reads as a designed frame rather than a letterbox. The 470px below the band becomes the caption zone — **captions never sit on the couple.** Tool: `scripts/assemble_short_hookcut.py`.
- **Smooth vertical Ken Burns = pre-upscale + animated `crop`, not `zoompan`.** Scale the source 1.10× past the band size, then translate the crop window with a linear `t` expression (`x='a+(b-a)*min(1,t/dur)'`). Pure translation has no zoompan frame-quantisation judder. Reserve `zoompan` for true push-ins on stills, and supersample 2× first.
- **Split lyric lines at the phrase comma for vertical karaoke.** A full sung line ("Tere bina main adhoora, tere bina main kho gaya") has to drop to ~40px to fit 1080 wide. Split it into two display lines in the *alignment source* — stable-ts then times each phrase independently — and both sweep at 58px. A 30s Short gets **six caption beats instead of three**, and each `wiperight` sweep stays semantically correct (a single sweep across a wrapped 2-line block fills both lines at once and reads wrong).
- **Cap the last sweep before the end card.** Whatever the aligner returns, hand-cap the final line so its gold fill completes *before* the card crossfades in — a sweep continuing over a title card looks like a bug. `scripts/lyric_karaoke.py` now takes `--fs` and `--yfrac` for exactly this vertical placement (defaults unchanged for 16:9).
- **Normalize the derivative to the same target as everything else (−14 LUFS / −1.5 TP).** A cut taken from a song's *final chorus* arrives ~3 LU hotter than one taken from its opening (−12.8 vs −15.8 measured here). Two-pass `loudnorm` on the **lossless wav before muxing**, then remux with `-c:v copy` — never re-encode the video to fix audio.

**Reusable craft from the Aaja Ve out-now glimpse Short (2026-08-07) — three silent ffmpeg failures that all report the correct duration:**
- 🔴 **A hard cut is NOT a very short `xfade`.** Modelling a cut as `xfade duration=1/fps` puts `offset+duration` exactly on the end of the incoming chain, so xfade has no frames left to blend: it emits **nothing from that join onward** and prints no warning. A 20.1s cut came out with 262 of its 603 video frames and still probed as 20.10s, because the container takes its duration from the **audio**. Structure the assembly instead as *groups*: consecutive shots joined by a hard cut are **concatenated**, and `xfade` is used only for real dissolves.
- 🔴 **The concat DEMUXER (`-f concat -c copy`) produces a file that breaks a downstream `xfade`.** The concatenated file itself is perfect — right frame count, right duration, plays fine — but fed into `xfade` it stops emitting at the first internal segment boundary. The concat **filter** (`concat=n=N:v=1:a=0`, re-encoding) does not. Measured side by side on identical inputs: demuxer 262 frames, filter 444. Costs one extra generation; take it.
- 🔴 **JPEG-backed and video-backed segments end up in different pixel formats and a mid-stream format change kills the filter chain.** Stills decode full-range and x264 tags them `yuvj420p`; a motion clip is `yuv420p`. Pin every segment with `scale=in_range=auto:out_range=tv` + `-color_range tv` so the whole cut is one format.
- **The common thread: `format=duration` is a claim about the longest stream, not about the video.** Any tool that assembles a timeline must **count video frames and compare against the predicted frame count**, and must re-probe every segment against the duration the timeline was built from (`zoompan` and `minterpolate` both land 1–4 frames short; end every segment chain in `tpad=stop_mode=clone` + an exact `-t`). All three bugs above were invisible to duration checks and caught in one line by a frame-count assertion.
- **True slow-motion for a short hero clip: `minterpolate` BEFORE `setpts`.** Stretching a 24fps 5.9s clip to 8.3s with `setpts` alone presents ~17 unique fps at 30 — visible judder. `minterpolate=fps=60:mi_mode=mci:mc_mode=aobmc:me_mode=bidir:vsbmc=1,setpts=N*PTS,fps=30` leaves every output frame unique; on a slow landscape shot with a walking figure it produced no tearing at the subject's hands or hair (checked at 6 timecodes before committing). ~30s of CPU for a 9s clip.
- **Duck to the `no_vocals` stem of the SAME timeline window, not to other music.** For an end card, crossfading the mix into a low-passed (`lowpass=f=820:poles=2`) `no_vocals` slice of the *identical bars* means the instrumental never changes — only the voice leaves. Measured: spectral centroid 4.8kHz → 420Hz, >1.5kHz energy 61% → 5%. A linear (`afade` default `tri`) crossfade between two correlated signals keeps the sum flat, so there is no level bump at the join.
- **Apple Color Emoji: the usable ppem is build-dependent.** The widely-cited 137 is **rejected** by this Pillow/FreeType build ("invalid pixel size") while 160 and 96 load. Try a list of ppem values and downscale, rather than hardcoding one — and verify the codepoint you actually rendered (U+1FAB7 is a lotus 🪷; the wilted rose is U+1F940 🥀).
- **Score the derivative gate, don't assert it.** dHash (64-bit, 0.5s sampling, Hamming ≤10 = same frame) over the new cut vs the live Short turns "<50% frame overlap" into a number — here 32.5%, concentrated entirely in the one shot the two cuts share. It also shows *where* the overlap is, which tells the next derivative what to avoid.
- **When several derivatives of one release are queued, write down what each one is NOT allowed to spend.** This cut left the final chorus, the verse scenes and full-bleed framing unspent and said so in the ledger, so the next cut has guaranteed room instead of discovering the collision at render time.
